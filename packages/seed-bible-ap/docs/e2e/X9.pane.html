Published about this verse

Notes publishers released about this verse — the people, places and practices it speaks of — each signed by its publisher and read here as published.

  • Aramaic-speaking Jewish followers of Jesus

    Believed Jesus was Israel’s Messiah. Many continued observing Jewish law and taking part in Jewish religious life. Acts 6:1 calls them “the Hebrews”, beside the Greek-speaking “Hellenists”.

    Where
    Jerusalem, Galilee and Judea; associated with Peter, James and the earliest disciples.
    Period
    from AD 30
    Writings
    Acts 1–6, 15 and 21; Galatians 2; the letter of James.
    Why these verses
    the Jerusalem church’s own life (Acts 2), the name “Hebrews” (Acts 6:1), the Torah-observing believers at the council (Acts 15) and the “many thousands … zealous for the law” (Acts 21:20), with the pillars James, Cephas and John (Gal 2:9).
    Sources
    Acts; Galatians; the Cambridge History of Christianity, vol. 1 (Origins to Constantine).

  • Greek-speaking Jewish Christians (the Hellenists)

    Jewish believers whose language and cultural background were Greek. Acts associates Stephen and Philip with this group, and it was Hellenists scattered after Stephen’s death who first preached to Greeks at Antioch.

    Where
    Jerusalem, then Jewish communities around the Mediterranean — Antioch, Cyprus, Cyrene.
    Period
    from AD 30
    Writings
    Acts 6–8, 9:29 and 11:19–26.
    Why these verses
    the name and the seven (Acts 6), the scattering after Stephen (Acts 8:1–5, 11:19), Saul’s dispute with the Hellenists (Acts 9:29) and the Antioch church they began (Acts 11:20–26).
    Sources
    Acts; Martin Hengel, Between Jesus and Paul (1983); the Cambridge History of Christianity, vol. 1.
