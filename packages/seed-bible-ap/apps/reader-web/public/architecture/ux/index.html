Architecture / UX

UX: what the reader sees

The new reader looks and reads like seedbible.org: the same screens, the same words, the same places for every button. What changed is underneath. Your marks go to your own Library, everything works on the device first, and every call the app makes, whether a tap, a question in words or another agent's request, goes to one workspace service that behaves one way.

Five principles

One backend for every call

A tap in the app, a question in words and another agent's request all reach the same workspace service and are handled the same way: one identity check, one named skill, one run record. There are not three backends.

Same as seedbible.org

Layout, wording and feature access match the old reader. No feature is dropped and none gets harder to reach.

Device first

A highlight shows the instant you make it, offline or signed out. It reaches your Library when you are signed in and online.

Sign-in is a Home ceremony

You sign in at your Home (www.faithnet.me), not with an emailed code to the app. The Home says what the reader may do for you.

Sharing is publishing

Your Library knows private and public. To let others see a note, you publish it to your public shelf. Nothing is public by default.

One backend for every call

The app's UI is a thin skin. Whatever happens on screen becomes a message to the reader's workspace agent, and the workspace behaves the same whoever is asking: the app after a tap, a person typing a question, or another agent over A2A. The workspace checks who is asking, plans one named skill with its arguments, runs it as that person through their own agent and the sources, and keeps a run reference. The UI never has a private path of its own.

Where each kind of call stands today

The acts already take this shape. Two kinds of call still enter by a side door on the same service, and the card does not yet tell another agent everything the workspace can do. The rule for every new call is the last column.

Call Today The rule
Acts: a highlight, a note, a work, a publish, a session, a friend, a picture The app sends a one-step plan naming the skill and its arguments; the workspace runs it on its harness as you and answers with a run reference. Seventeen skills, one list. This is the shape.
Reads: a chapter, a verse's content, places, search, presence, a public shelf Plain routes on the same service with the same identity check, but not skills: not on the card, not recorded as runs, not reachable by another agent. Each read becomes a skill; its route stays only as an alias of that skill.
An ask in words: the study companion A route that relays the question to the Seed registry's model, a different behaviour from every other call. A workspace skill that asks the Scripture agent's harness, planned and recorded like any act.
Another agent The door is open (the card, A2A messages, the harness), but the card advertises one skill and the planner knows only that one, so a message in words cannot yet reach the other sixteen. The card lists every skill; a message in words is planned over the same list the app uses.

Why it matters. With one backend, a feature is built once and is at once a button, a sentence and a skill another agent can call. A test of the skill is a test of all three. And a reader can see every call in one run record, whoever made it.

How a tap becomes a record

This is why the reader feels instant and works on a plane. It is also why nothing of yours is lost when you sign in on a new device: the reader pulls your Library and merges it with what the device already has, so neither copy overwrites the other.

Signing in

  1. You press Sign in with Faithnet. A small window opens at your Home.
  2. You continue with Google or your email. New people get their account and their own agent there.
  3. The first time, the Home makes sure your agent carries the reader's behaviour, and gives the reader's workspace its "ask as me" permission.
  4. The window closes. Your marks from this device move into your Library. Your sign-in lasts as long as your Home's; when it ends, the reader says so and signs you out too.

How the experience is checked

Every feature has a flow test: a script that opens the live site in a real browser, does what a person would do (tap, type, wait), takes a screenshot at each step, and checks what appears. Running the suite writes ✅ or ❌ into the Test column of the training manual, so the feature list always says what actually works today on seed-reader.faithnet.io.
