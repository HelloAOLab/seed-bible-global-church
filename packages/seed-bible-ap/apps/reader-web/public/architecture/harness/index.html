Scripture harness: how a question is answered from evidence

When someone asks the Scripture agent a question such as "who were the sons of God in Genesis 6?", the answer is not written from the model's memory. It is built from evidence the agent holds: the verses from a signed corpus, the manuscripts and versions, the writings of the passage's own time, and what later readers made of it. This page shows every source that feeds the answer, the seven steps that produce it, what comes out, and the checks that keep it honest.

The sources of content

Every source is held under its own rights and in its own place. Three things are true of all of them: the text sits in a signed record, the rights are recorded per edition, and the harness reads them through indexes it can rebuild at any time.

Rights decide three things. Whether a source is published as a work (an unlicensed text gets a record with its title and date, and nothing more), whether it is indexed and in which partition, and whether an answer may quote it. The Reformation Study Bible's notes are held by permission in a partition that is never exported, and no claim is ever extracted from them.

How a source becomes evidence

  1. Snapshot. The source's bytes are pinned outside every repository with their hash, the edition and the licence with its evidence.
  2. Publish. One work per edition on Source Publishing, one signed block per citable unit: a verse of 1 Enoch, a section of Josephus, one comment of Rashi on one verse, a paragraph of Augustine, a study note. A block is placed on a verse only by a citation in its own text or its editor's notes, or by a reviewed link in the research atlas. Nothing is placed by guess, and a verse outside the canon is a refusal, not a silent miss.
  3. Index. The indexer reads the published works back and writes six projections: by verse, by name, by source word, by meaning, the chapter record and the book windows. It is the only writer. Each unit carries the work's date with the basis for that date, its licence, its partition and the link to the signed block. Wiping an index loses nothing a rebuild does not restore.
  4. Layer. Code decides, against the asked book's window, whether a writing is of the passage's own period or a later reading. A work with no date is served as undated, never placed.

Why not a packaged retrieval product? The unit of retrieval here must be one signed block with its locator, so a hit can be verified against the gateway. Packaged products split files into overlapping chunks that straddle blocks, rewrite the query, and write their own answer. The meaning index is one lane into the pool, with the same discipline as the other five.

The seven steps

Each step is recorded as its own receipt under the run. A step that fails returns the failure, and every later step passes it on, so the asker reads the cause rather than a thinner answer.

  1. Classify (model, Haiku). The question's kind and its anchors: verses, source words at a verse, names, passages of writings named by their own reference, and search phrasings. Code validates every anchor and drops the rest by name: a verse id must parse and be in the canon, a Strong's number must actually occur at the verse it is claimed for, a name must be one the index holds.
  2. Gather (code, in parallel). The verses resolved from the signed corpus in the asker's edition, each commitment verified; the writings that cite the verse or that the atlas relates to it; the chapter record; the scroll copies and quotations and the Septuagint at the verse; every verse a source word occurs in; the writings that name the anchors; and the meaning index, once per layer. One pool of evidence items, each with an id, a kind, a layer decided by code, its date with its basis, its licence and where it is published. The pool is capped per layer (40 text, 30 period, 30 later, 12 register) and every cut is counted. A channel that fails answers nothing and is named in the record; the gather goes on with the others.
  3. Select (model, Opus). The model sees ids and one-line summaries only, never the full text, and returns an outline whose every point names its evidence ids. A verse it wants that is not in the pool is resolved and verified like any other, or listed as not used.
  4. Compose (model, Opus). The model sees the selected items whole and writes claims, each with its layer, its kind (says, attests, interprets, disputes) and its evidence ids, then the prose and a next step.
  5. Check (code). A claim citing no held item is struck. A year, century or BC/AD span in the prose that no cited item's date carries is struck: the chronology dates, the model never does. A claim about what the text says that cites a later writing is struck. A quotation not found in a held item is struck. Nothing struck is hidden.
  6. Revise (model, once). Only when the check refused something: the draft once more, told exactly why. A second refusal stands.
  7. Answer (code). The final check and the record: the prose, the claims, the citations, the evidence grouped by layer and sorted by date, what was struck, what was proposed but not used, and the coverage counts.

Robustness: checks, not intentions

  • The model never supplies a verse, a date or a quotation. Verses come from the signed corpus and are commitment-verified. Dates come only from the chronology. A quotation must be in a held item whose edition allows quoting.
  • Layers are decided by code. Period or later is a comparison of the work's date with the book's window, done at query time. The model never labels a writing.
  • Three things stay visibly apart: what the text says, what writings of its own time say, and what later readers made of it. Where faithful readers differ, the answer names the disagreement and does not resolve it.
  • One mechanism per channel. The meaning index has one fixed floor and no second, looser one; a thin pool is reported as thin, never padded.
  • A failed channel is named, not hidden. A tool that errors contributes nothing and is listed under dropped; a failed step returns its cause and every later step carries it to the asker.
  • Every cut is counted. Verses past the cap, items past a layer's cap, Hebrew twins of a comment also held in English, names the knowledge base does not hold: all appear in the record.
  • Rights are enforced at three points: at publish (the lane), at index (the partition in the vector's metadata, filtered per lane), and at answer (quote only where the edition allows it, checked on the signed block).
  • Nothing here reaches the translation drafter. The writings index has no binding in the translation runtime, and a continuous-integration check says so. Later readings, including the study notes, never inform a translation decision.
  • Everything is a projection. The works are the record. The indexes are rebuilt from them, and the run record names the work versions it read, so an answer can be reproduced.
  • Every step is a receipt. The run is a recorded plan on the agent; the asker can read what each step received and returned.

How it is measured

A set of ten cases, each with its gold evidence by id and a reference answer, is run in arms: the older single-skill ask, the harness without the meaning channel, the meaning channel alone, and the whole harness, three fresh runs each. Deterministic graders score evidence recall per layer, citation coverage, date provenance (the target is every date carried by a cited item), layer discipline, quotation rights and which channel found the required items. A judge model scores whether each claim follows from its evidence. The default switches only on a lift in recall with date provenance intact.

Where it stands, 26 October 2026. The harness is live on the Scripture agent and answers the dev set in 60 to 140 seconds a case; the latest run over the rebuilt indexes answered all twenty cases, with every claim cited and every date carried. The indexes now cover 119 published works in 65 editions, 224,799 units: the Sefaria commentaries, the Ante-Nicene Fathers I to V, Philo, Jubilees and the Pseudepigrapha, Eusebius, the ancient Near Eastern and Greek sources the writings cite, and the non-biblical Dead Sea Scroll lines. The Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers and Ante-Nicene volumes VI to IX are publishing and enter at the next rebuild. The study notes are published as their own works and are not in the writings indexes. The translation workbench asks the harness today; the reader's Companion still asks the Seed registry's model about the chapter on screen, and wiring it to this record is next.
