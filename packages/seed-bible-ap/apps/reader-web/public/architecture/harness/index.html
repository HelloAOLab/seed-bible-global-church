Architecture / Agentic / Scripture harness

Scripture harness: how a question is answered from evidence

When someone asks the Scripture agent a question, the answer is not written from a model's memory. It is built from evidence the agent holds: the verse from a signed corpus, its source words, the story around it, what the knowledge base knows about the people and places in it, what this translation decided there and why, the manuscripts and versions, the writings of the passage's own time, and what later readers made of it. Code decides what is evidence, which layer it belongs to, and which dates it may give. A model only relates the evidence to the question. Code then checks the answer and strikes what does not hold. There are two ways to ask, and only two: ask-verse, fast, about a verse you have open, and ask-writings, broad, about anything the agent holds. They are different harnesses with different designs, and this page keeps them apart. A question gets ask-writings unless it is anchored to a verse. (ask-scripture, the first general ask, was retired on 29 October 2026 and is refused by name.)

Chosen when The ask is anchored to a verse, or to up to five verses of one chapter, that the asker names. Every other ask. This is the default: an ask that names no skill and no verse is an ask-writings ask.
For A question about one verse you have open (or up to five): "what does this word mean?", "who is 'he' here?", "why does this translation read it so?", "what do the scrolls have?". An open question over everything the agent holds: a theme, how a reading changed over time, a writing by name, the collection itself, this edition's choices. The translation workbench's Ask, and any question the fast ask hands on.
Evidence The verse's dossier, built once from records the system already holds and stored. Nothing is searched at ask time. Gathered for the question at ask time from fifteen channels: verses, citations, names linked to the knowledge base, meaning (writings and Scripture), exact words, the edition's record, the catalog.
Model One streamed call to Claude Haiku 5.5, with the dossier as a cached prefix. Haiku 5.5 classifies; the agent's model (Claude Opus 5.5) selects the evidence and writes the answer.

Checked by code The same rules on both: every claim cites held evidence; no date a cited item does not carry; a later reading never stands for what the text says; no quotation the evidence does not hold. The open ask also strikes kinship the knowledge base contradicts, and an "edition decides" claim about a question the edition leaves open.

A verse question that reaches beyond the verse ("where else in the Bible…", "throughout the prophets") is not guessed at. The fast ask says so in one sentence and offers the open ask as a button, and the slow search runs only when the reader presses it.

Which ask answers

One small function decides, in code, from what the request says. No model chooses between the asks.

The request Answered by
A plain ask, with or without a role and a study context, naming no verse ask-writings (the default)
A plain ask whose context names a verse (context.reference: John.2.4) or a range of at most five in one chapter (John.2.1-5, or context.verses listing consecutive verses) ask-verse
A range of more than five verses, scattered verses, or verses from two chapters ask-writings. The fast ask reads a verse or five, and it will not quietly keep the first five of nine.
The skill or mode named: ask-verse / ask-writings The one named. Naming ask-verse without a verse is refused, and a named ask-writings wins over a verse in the context.
ask-scripture, or a mode nobody defined Refused, with the reason. It is not answered by another ask in its place.

ask-scripture proposed passages from memory, resolved them through the corpus and composed an answer. It had no writings, no edition record and no knowledge base behind it, and on the first open-ask gold set it reached required recall 0.35 against the open ask's 0.77. It stays in the measurements below as the baseline, and nowhere else. A request that cannot run is told so: the old route fell back to ask-scripture whenever the open ask could not run, and that fall-back is gone.

The picture

Layers of evidence

Every item of evidence carries a layer, decided by code, never by the model. The layer says what an item may be used for.

Layer Holds May say what the text says?
text The verse and its neighbours from the signed corpus, in the reader's edition; its source words (Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek) with their glosses; the Septuagint; the Dead Sea Scroll copies; parallel passages. Yes. Only this layer may.
edition What this translation decided at the verse and why: its note on the source, its interpretation record (the reading it adopted, the alternatives it keeps, the questions it leaves open), its curated concept rules. No. Told as "this edition reads…"
reference Knowledge-base facts (who a name denotes, kin, ancestry, a place and where it is) and the story layer (the scene, who is on stage, who "he" is). Each names its source: TIPNR, OpenBible, a curated file, or the story layer, which says when it is not yet reviewed. No. Reference data, never a witness.
period Writings dated inside the book's window: of the passage's own time. Scroll quotations of the verse. No. Told as theirs.
later Writings dated after the window: reception (the Fathers, the rabbis, the Targums, the Reformers). No. Told as reception.
register · undated The owner's chronology register entries; works the chronology does not date (served as undated, never placed). No.

The verse ask (ask-verse)

Harness 1 of 2. A function of one verse: stored evidence, one streamed call, seconds. The trained-model design further down does not change it.

The reader already knows the verse, so nothing is classified and nothing is searched. The evidence for a verse is a function of the verse, so it is built once and stored: the verse dossier (ap.scripture.verse-dossier.v1), one per edition and verse.

The dossier: a join and a trim, never model-written

Section From
verse The verse in the reader's edition from the signed corpus, with its commitment; for the RBS, its stage (every check passes, or a check is open) and its note on the source.
context Two verses each side, same edition.
story The story layer's state at the verse: the scene and its span, the mode (narration, speech), the speaker, who is on stage with natural gender and number, who each reference in the verse is, the speech acts.
meaning The meaning layer for the scene: what it does and what it leaves open, the connectors and question words at the verse, the book's key words that occur in it.
source The source words in order with their Strong's numbers and glosses; a curated concept rule wherever one binds a word.
knowledgeBase For each person and place the story layer binds at the verse: description, period, parents, partner, children, tribe, modern name.
interpretation The edition's interpretation record: each question, its status, the reading decided with its rationale and footnote, the alternatives kept.
witnesses The Septuagint (Swete); which Dead Sea Scrolls copy the verse; which scroll lines quote it; parallel passages.
writings Units placed on the verse by citation (at most two a work, eight a layer, the layer against the book's window, each date with its basis); the chapter's period and later writings from the chronology.
register The chronology register's entries at the verse.

A dossier is about 7,000 to 14,000 characters. It is built on the first ask of a verse (0.3 to 1.7 s, every source is in the same D1) and read in about 40 ms afterwards; reloading any source empties the store, so the next ask rebuilds it. The other English Bibles' wordings of the verse (WEB, ASV, KJV, BSB) ride beside the dossier for the leak check only and never enter a prompt.

Five steps, each timed on the receipt

  1. Dossier (code). Read the stored dossier, or build and store it. A range of up to five verses reads each one and keeps the first whole.
  2. Focus (code). The question's words point at sections: a word or "mean" at the source words, "who" or a pronoun at the story and the knowledge base, "why does this translation" at the edition's record, "scroll" or "Septuagint" at the witnesses, "Fathers" or "rabbis" at later writings. A question that names another chapter, asks "where else" or "throughout", or names a writing the dossier does not hold is off-verse: one sentence, and a next step to the open ask.
  3. Compose (Haiku 5.5, streamed). The system prompt and the whole dossier are a cached prefix: the same bytes for every question about the verse, so a follow-up reads them from the cache. The focus is an instruction after it. Items are tagged [E1]…[En], and every claim cites its tags. Thinking is off; the dossier is the expertise.
  4. Check (code). Over the finished text, sentence by sentence: an unknown tag; a later reading given as what the text says; a date no cited item carries; a quotation of seven words or more that no item holds; five words in a row from another English Bible's wording of the verse that the dossier itself does not hold; a pronoun resolution the story layer contradicts. A failing sentence is struck in place, with its reason.
  5. Answer. The receipt: the dossier version, the model, the skill and its hash, what was cited and struck, cache reads and writes, timings.

Transport. The agent answers the A2A streaming method with server-sent events: who (agent, model, skill), dossier (the items, so citations can render as the text arrives), delta (text), check (what was struck) and done (the receipt, with the checked answer). SendMessage answers the same thing whole. The Seed reader's /companion/ask calls this skill over the standard A2A client it already uses for every Bible read, and pipes the stream to the pane. The pane shows each source by number, the struck sentences struck through with the reason on hover, and the agent, model and skill that answered. Under each answer the pane lists what to read next, chosen by code: the passage before and after the verse (by the Berean text's own section breaks, named by the story layer), the parallel passages of the verse and of what follows (Matthew 11:1 reads on to 10:1–42, 11:2–19 and Luke 7:18–20), then the strongest cross-references; and two or three follow-up questions the verse's evidence can answer. Answers run about 120–170 words: the direct answer, then up to four points. The earlier companion asked a small open model about the chapter text through a shared secret, with no evidence at all; it is out of the path.

The open ask (ask-writings)

Harness 2 of 2, and the default. Evidence gathered per question, a pipeline of recorded steps, minutes. It is the ask the design work in "Next" targets.

Seven recorded steps on the agent, each its own receipt. A step that fails returns its cause, and every later step carries it to the asker.

  1. Classify (Haiku 5.5). The question's kind (text, history of reading, term, source, compare, verse, collection, edition) and its anchors: verses, source words at a verse, names, passages of writings by their own reference, exact phrases, search phrasings. Code validates every anchor: a verse must be in the canon, a Strong's number must occur at the verse claimed, a quoted phrase in the question is taken as an exact-words search whatever the model named.
  2. Link (code). Every name is linked to the knowledge-base people, places and things it can denote. The story layer's binding at the question's verses decides first (which John), then a single candidate; otherwise the name stays ambiguous, every candidate kept and none chosen.
  3. Gather (code, in parallel). Only the graph queries the question's skill declares (below). Channels: the verses, resolved and verified in the asker's edition; Scripture by meaning (verses the model did not name); writings citing each verse; the chapter's writings record; scroll copies and quotations and the Septuagint; every verse a source word occurs in; writings naming a linked referent, by every name it has; writings named by their own reference; writings by meaning, per layer; writings by exact words; the edition's record at each verse; the knowledge-base facts on each referent; concept rules; and, for a question about the collection, the chronology's compositions and the catalog. A meaning hit that names a linked person as someone else is dropped and counted. Each layer is capped, with at most eight items of one work, and every cut is counted.
  4. Select (Opus 5.5). Sees one-line summaries only and returns an outline whose every point names its evidence. The answering steps are told what the agent holds (the chronology's 286 writings in Ask, in 578 catalog works and 242,763 passages) and may never say a held writing is missing; one not retrieved is "not retrieved for this question". If a layer the pool holds is ignored, the outline is asked for once more.
  5. Compose (Opus 5.5). Sees the selected items whole and writes claims, each with its layer, its kind and its evidence, then the prose.
  6. Check (code). Strikes a claim citing nothing held; a date no cited item carries; a "what the text says" claim resting on anything but the text layer; a quotation not held; a kinship sentence ("X's father was Y", "Y, the son of X") the knowledge base contradicts for a linked person; a claim that the edition decides a question its own record leaves open.
  7. Revise, then answer. Once, only if the check struck something, told why. A second refusal stands, and what was struck is reported.

Each skill is a SKILL.md in the skills repository whose frontmatter lists its queries. The export compiles them into the agent and refuses a query no projection serves, and preflight refuses an agent module the skills have moved past.

The knowledge graph, projected

The ontology lives in GraphDB, and the repository holds its source. The ask never queries GraphDB. GraphDB Free runs two queries at once, so one slow query is an outage, and its reasoner once filled the disk through a single symmetric, transitive relation. Instead, each question the ask may put to the graph is a named query, answered by a table built at build time from the same sources. Every closure is computed in code (all of a person's ancestors, every name a referent is called by), every row names its source, and preflight rebuilds the projection from the sources and refuses drift.

Rights decide three things. Whether a source is published as a work (an unlicensed text gets a record with its title and date, and nothing more), whether it is indexed and in which partition, and whether an answer may quote it. The study Bible's notes are held by permission in a partition that is never exported, and no claim is ever extracted from them.

The publishing path, the writings ontology, and the programme that drafts English for writings with none are on their own page: Period writings.

How a source becomes evidence

  1. Snapshot. The source's bytes are pinned outside every repository with their hash, the edition and the licence with its evidence.
  2. Publish. One work per edition on Source Publishing, one signed block per citable unit: a verse of 1 Enoch, a section of Josephus, one comment of Rashi on one verse, a paragraph of Augustine, a study note. A block is placed on a verse only by a citation in its own text or its editor's notes, or by a reviewed link in the research atlas. Nothing is placed by guess, and a verse outside the canon is a refusal, not a silent miss.
  3. Index. The indexer reads the published works back and writes its projections: by verse, by referent, by source word, by meaning, by exact words, the chapter record and the book windows; a reload empties the stored verse dossiers so each is rebuilt from what was just loaded. It is the only writer. Each unit carries the work's date with the basis for that date, its licence, its partition and the link to the signed block. Wiping an index loses nothing a rebuild does not restore.
  4. Layer. Code decides, against the asked book's window, whether a writing is of the passage's own period or a later reading. A work with no date is served as undated, never placed.

Robustness: checks, not intentions

  • The model never supplies a verse, a date or a quotation. Verses come from the signed corpus in the reader's edition; dates only from the chronology, each with its basis; a quotation must be in held evidence.
  • Layers are decided by code. Period or later is the work's date against the book's window. Edition and reference are kinds of record. The model never labels evidence.
  • A name is linked by rule, and ambiguity is kept. The story layer at the verse, then a single candidate; otherwise every candidate is carried and the answer is told not to choose.
  • The graph is a projection, never a live dependency. No SPARQL at ask time; closures computed in code; preflight holds the projection to its sources.
  • The agent knows what it holds. It may not call a held writing missing, and a count it gives says what it counts.
  • No other English Bible's wording enters an answer about a verse. The check strikes five words in a row from the WEB, ASV, KJV or BSB at that verse unless the dossier itself holds them (the reader's own verse, or a writing quoting it).
  • A failed channel is named, not hidden. It contributes nothing and is listed; the gather goes on. A heavy load of the data worker's database makes its reads fail; loads run between measurements, never during them.
  • Every cut is counted, including verses past the cap, items past a layer's cap, a second language copy of a comment, a meaning hit naming the wrong person, and a dossier section over its size target.
  • Rights are enforced three times: at publish (the lane), at index (the partition, filtered per lane) and at answer (quote only where the edition allows it).
  • Nothing here reaches the translation drafter. Later readings and the edition's own decisions never inform a translation decision.

How it is measured

Each ask has its own development sets, written as drafts for the owner to adjudicate. The gold names the evidence a complete answer cites. Graders are code (recall of the gold, citation coverage, date provenance, layer discipline, leaks, story agreement, struck rate, latency). Every case runs three times in a fresh conversation, and results from different sets or judges are never pooled. Next: the trained model in ask-writings (design, 29 October 2026)

Built the same day, not yet deployed: the retrieval compile, the writings evaluation runner, and the Christian Ask workbench. The design specification remains ask-writings design. Haiku is still the writer. The table below is not the next step. Models of our own for the drafting, query and story steps are described on Our own models in the harness. A draft redesign of retrieval, open for external review: ask-writings v2 design.

Status: a design, not a release. The ancient-en model is a QLoRA fine-tune (rank 16) of Qwen3.5-9B trained on aligned source-language passages and their English (Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, Latin). It is built for translation. Its ask task (the a1 row: a question, the evidence the live harness selected, and a cited answer the code accepted) is written and distilled on a probe; no ask row has trained yet, and nothing below is measured on this model. Each item says what is built, what is proposed, and the number that decides whether it ships. This model never enters the study-Bible loop, and the study Bible's notes stay in their partition that is never exported.

Scope: ask-writings only. It is the default ask and the one that gathers evidence per question, so it is where a model that reads the original languages and runs cheaply helps most. ask-verse keeps Haiku 5.5 and its stored dossier; it is not redesigned here, and a verse-anchored ask is routed to it before any of this applies.

ask-writings is already retrieval-augmented: code gathers evidence from fifteen channels and a model relates it to the question. So the trained model does not add RAG. It changes who does each job in the pipeline, and it opens jobs that a general model does poorly or too expensively: reading the original languages, running the same small step thousands of times, and answering in seconds instead of minutes. The design keeps every rule the page already states. The invariants come first.

Invariants the model does not change

  • Code decides what is evidence, its layer and its dates. A model never assigns a layer, and never supplies a date.
  • A model proposes; code gates. Anything the trained model produces that could be wrong in a decidable way (a cited id, a quoted span, a date, a relation) is checked by code, and a failure is struck, not argued with.
  • GraphDB is never asked at ask time. The graph reaches the ask only as projected tables. Everything graph-shaped below is a new projection or a new named query over one, declared in a skill, never a live SPARQL call.
  • The model is a port. Each job below names the model that does it today, the trained model as a candidate, and the measurement that moves the job. The route (Workers AI, RunPod serverless, the merged export) can change without the harness changing.
  • Items are evidence, not instructions. The training rows and the live prompt both say so; a writing that tells the reader to do something is quoted, never obeyed.

Classify, anchors Haiku 5.5, anchors validated by code Candidate. A short, high-volume step; the 9B is cheap enough to run twice and compare. Same anchors accepted by code on the 20 gold cases as Haiku.

Graph-grounded retrieval, without asking GraphDB

Today the graph reaches ask-writings as named queries answered by projected tables (kb.link, kb.kin, kb.concept and the rest), and the ontology's people, places and things are linked to a question by code. The trained model makes a richer use of the same graph possible, by reading it as text.

  1. Fact cards. Project each referent's one- and two-hop neighbourhood (typed edge, direction, the source that asserts it, its IRI) as short cards in a kb_card table, built at build time with the rest of the projection. The composer reads [kb:person/…] Nathan —prophet-to→ David (source: …) as it reads any item. The card's layer is kb, decided by code, and its claims are as strong as the edge's own source, not stronger.
  2. Walks as declared queries. New named queries a skill can list in its frontmatter: kb.path (the shortest chain between two linked referents, at most three hops), kb.neighbourhood, kb.concept-family (a concept with its narrower and related terms from the ontology's T-box) and kb.timeline (a referent against the dated writings that name it). Each is a table lookup with a budget, refused at export if no projection serves it, like every other query.
  3. Ontology-widened search. A question about a concept searches the concept and its narrower terms, in English and in the original-language forms the ontology records. The expansion is a join in code; the trained model only adds phrasings the ontology does not list, and each added phrasing is marked as the model's.
  4. Relations are checked the way kinship is. The check that strikes a kinship sentence the knowledge base contradicts generalises: a composed claim carries typed about referents and, where it states a relation, the relation's name. Code strikes a relation the cards contradict. A relation the cards do not hold is not struck, because the graph is not complete; it is marked not in the knowledge base.

Where to take it: seven ideas, each with its gate

Idea What it does Gate before it ships
1. A bridge to the originals An English question is expanded into Hebrew, Greek and Latin search forms. Hits in the original-language text are shown with their English, the held translation if there is one, otherwise a labelled draft. A reader who knows no Greek finds what only the Greek reaches. Gold items reached only through an expanded phrase, over the 20 gold cases and the Christian Ask sets; no loss on the rest.
2. Reading what has no English Of the 578 catalog works, those with no English can be read at ask time: a bounded gloss of the few passages the question reaches, cached by passage and model version, layer draft, dated from the work and never from the model, never quoted as period English. The ask says it is a machine reading. The translation programme's own gate (checks over chrF), a reader-visible label, and a cap on drafts per answer.
3. Two writers, compared by code On the same selected evidence, the 9B and the larger model both compose. Code compares which items each cites and which claims each makes. Agreement serves the 9B's answer; a divergence goes to the larger model and is logged. It is the translation harness's rule: spend on a trigger, not on every row. The share of asks answered by the 9B alone, at no loss of required recall against the larger model, and a lower cost per answer.
4. Extractive grounding For each claim the model proposes the exact span of the cited item that supports it. Code checks the span is in the item, verbatim. A claim whose span is not there is struck as unsupported, and the span can be shown on tap. Struck-for-no-support recall on a set of planted unsupported claims, and no rise in the false-strike rate.
5. Speak first, deepen on request The open ask is minutes. With a model that composes in seconds, a checked first answer streams from the 9B in the first moments, with a go deeper button that runs the full open ask, the same pattern the verse ask already uses for a question that reaches beyond the verse. First-words and done times at p95, and the share of readers whose first answer was enough (no press of the button).
6. Ask as discovery A checked answer states relations. Those the knowledge base does not hold become candidate edges with their evidence, queued for the workbench, never written to the ontology automatically. An expert accepts or rejects them, so the answers that are used to read the graph also grow it. Accept rate of candidate edges over a month, and none reaches the graph without a person's decision.
7. A flywheel that cannot leak Every code-clean answer from the live asks is a candidate training row, with its evidence ids and rights recorded. The next training pass takes only rows whose evidence is cleared for training, groups by work family so a family never sits in two splits, and is measured on the Christian Ask Workbench and the ask-writings gold before it serves. Zero rows from the study Bible partition, from non-commercial machine English or from programme translations; the held-out gold stays held out; the model card carries the share-alike terms.

What the page's numbers say about where to start

On the 20-case gold set the open ask moved from required recall 0.658 to 0.767 when names were linked to the graph, the edition layer was added and the index widened; the verse ask is at 0.971 in under 3 seconds. Retrieval and the checks, not the writer, were what moved the number. That points the first work at the parts the trained model is best at and the writer cannot fix: idea 1 (more of the right evidence reaches the writer), idea 4 (a stronger check) and the graph cards. Composing with the 9B (idea 3) comes after, because a cheaper writer on weaker evidence only answers faster.

Serving it

Three routes are measured against each other on the same rows: a Worker calling the merged model on RunPod serverless (the model exists; it costs a standing charge to avoid a 30 to 60 second cold start), a stock Workers AI model with no fine-tune (about $0.0006 an ask, no GPU), and a retrain on a base Workers AI accepts for adapters. The decision rule is the translation epoch's: if the stock model is within the paired bootstrap of the fine-tune on the same rows, nothing of ours needs a GPU. The retrieval (D1, Vectorize with bge-m3), the cards and the checks stay in the Worker whichever route wins.

How it is measured

  • Equal-budget arms on the same cases: the larger model composing; the 9B composing; the 9B with expanded queries; the two writers compared. A stage must beat the arm that spends as much.
  • Two instruments. The ask-writings gold (required recall, date provenance, layer discipline) and the Christian Ask sets, judged on five dimensions by a judge that stays the same across arms. Numbers from different judges are never pooled.
  • Honest about contamination. The gold asks have not yet been rewritten, so a measure on them is a shape test until they are. Training asks are grouped by work family, and the gold works are held out.
  • A model that does not know declines. Ten percent of rows retrieve nothing relevant and the completion says so. Without them, retrieval-augmented answering becomes confabulation with footnotes. The negatives are not built yet.
