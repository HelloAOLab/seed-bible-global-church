ask-writings v3: knowledge prepared before the question, a plan shaped by it

Specification, for review. Date: 1 November 2026 (revision 2, after an outside review). Status: proposed. It answers the planning brief: doctrine, interpretation and chronology do not reach Ask; almost every question triggers the same large search; and the answer rules are better at preventing unsupported statements than at enabling complete, well-supported explanations. The central move: turn the ontology and the writings into reusable, source-backed knowledge prepared before a question arrives — attributed propositions with the arguments that connect them to their passages — and let one harness assemble it with a small plan compiled for the question. Revision 2 changes are marked (rev 2).

1. Summary

  1. Retrieve propositions and their supporting arguments, not only passages (rev 2: the strongest change). A doctrine answer retrieves the position, who holds it, the exact supporting spans, the stated connection between them, its qualifications and alternatives, and its provenance — always with the passages themselves.
  2. Prepare evidence bundles before the question (rev 2): per passage, doctrine, author-and-topic and historical interpretation; versioned, rebuilt by recorded dependency; built offline on RunPod.
  3. One harness, a plan compiled per question: parameterised templates per question family over a small library of typed operations; a model proposes changes only when a template does not fit; code validates types, dependencies, permissions and budgets.
  4. Claim types with their own validation (rev 2): "this passage says", "this author teaches", "this tradition interprets", "taken together these suggest", "these total", "no earlier example was found" — each supported and checked differently.
  5. Explicit perspective (rev 2): reader role, theological perspective and question purpose are separate; a named source or tradition sets the perspective; a disputed question with none named gets the positions explained.
  6. A question-form index and contextual retrieval (rev 2), tested on a limited collection first.
  7. Skills as executable, versioned specifications, improved offline from traces against held-out questions.
  8. Our own models for bounded, measurable jobs: offline extraction first.
  9. Two evaluation tracks (rev 2): source-grounded explanation and independent synthesis, with source exclusion following the task, paired on the same question ids, and the judge calibrated against blinded human review.

2. Principles

  1. Prepared knowledge plans and explains; held text proves. A proposition record supplies the explanation's structure; the answer still cites the passages.
  2. Models propose, code decides — frames, plans, extracted records, claims. Code validates references, types, arithmetic, permissions and attribution. Code cannot establish that an interpretation is true because its evidence ids exist: semantic support needs reviewed relationships or a calibrated assessment.
  3. One harness. A question changes the plan, never the system.
  4. Extend, don't duplicate. The doctrine context already holds propositions, commitments, chain propositions and defeat conditions; the interpretation layer holds questions, candidates and reception claims. Expose them first, then add only the missing supporting relationships.
  5. Every record has provenance and a review status; model-generated interpretations keep theirs at runtime.
  6. Less reconstruction per request. The speed gain comes from doing the expensive work during ingestion, not from cutting verification.

3. Propositions with their arguments (rev 2)

The record Ask retrieves for a position. It extends sdo:DoctrineProposition and the interpretation layer's candidates; it is not a parallel ontology.

Part What it supplies Term (existing → added)
Proposition the position stated sdo:DoctrineProposition, sdo:ChainProposition (exist)
Attribution the author, work or tradition advancing it; endorsing vs reporting vs rejecting sdo:AdoptionAssertion, sdo:DoctrinalCommitment (exist) + sdo:stance (endorses · reports · rejects · qualifies)
Supporting spans the exact source spans used + sdo:SupportLink → txt:Span / verse
The connection how those spans support the position — the step a list of proof texts leaves the model to reconstruct + sdo:Argument (premises, inference stated, the span each premise rests on)
Qualifications conditions and limits + sdo:Qualification
Alternatives relevant disagreement sdo:DoctrineRelationAssessment, interpretation candidates (exist)
Defeat conditions what would undermine it (a disputed date, two writings treated as independent witnesses) sdo:DefeatCondition (exists) — used to drive targeted checks (§6)
Context speaker, tradition, date uncertainty, source dependence chronology, writings ontology (exist)
Provenance and review where the record came from, how it was checked prov:wasDerivedFrom, review status

Retrieval: for "what does the Westminster Shorter Catechism teach about X" the harness retrieves that catechism's position and its supports directly (source-grounded track, §13); for an open question it retrieves the relevant attributed positions and how they relate. Indexing propositions as retrieval units, and searching paths through context-rich propositions, follows Dense X Retrieval and PropRAG (§16), adapted to keep speaker, stance, tradition, dates and dependence.

First experiment: a small, representative set of doctrinal topics; the composer held fixed; passage retrieval vs proposition-plus-evidence retrieval on independently authored questions.

4. Prepared evidence bundles (rev 2)

Bundle Contents Built from
Passage context, speakers, referents, scene, significant wording, cross-references with relation, open interpretation questions, the edition's notes story layer, verse dossier (exists), OpenBible / TSK cross-references, interpretation layer
Doctrine attributed propositions, arguments, qualifications, alternative positions, proof spans doctrine context, held confessions and Fathers
Author × topic the author's position, the relevant passages, the spans writings index, citation index, propositions
Historical interpretation dated attestations, transmission relationships, competing date claims reference chronology, writings ontology, reception claims
  • Bundles hold evidence and explanatory structure; the answer is still composed for the reader's actual question.
  • GraphDB stays the authoritative relationship store; bundles are compact, versioned projections in the runtime store (D1); a change to a source or annotation rebuilds the bundles that depend on it.
  • Extraction, alignment, candidate linking and bundle building run offline on RunPod (a fixed cost).
  • A pronoun question reads the passage bundle; it does not run fifteen search channels to rediscover the scene. Graph-integrated retrieval precedent: HippoRAG 2 (§16).

5. One harness, a plan per question

5.1 The question frame

Classify fills a typed frame (validated by SHACL): family (doctrine, precept, passage explanation, reference/pronoun, word study, person or place, history of reading, comparison, source, pastoral, apologetic, factual), subject (ontology nodes, resolved by code), aspect, answer form, perspective (§7) and depth. (rev 2) The planner sees only the question, the relevant conversation, a concise capability catalog and the selected family's instructions — not every skill and rule (today's classify call is 16k tokens, mostly a works catalog).

5.2 The operation library

Operation Purpose Backed by
Resolve reference the exact work, passage, entity or concept resolve, KB, cited-form resolution (exist)
Read passage or bundle primary text and necessary context passage text, verse dossier, bundles (§4)
Retrieve positions attributed propositions or interpretations proposition index (§3), interpretation layer
Follow supporting links the spans and arguments behind a position sdo:SupportLink, sdo:Argument
Compare agreements and differences between named positions relation assessments, compatibility
Check chronology date claims and source dependence chronology, writings ontology
Search an unresolved requirement fill a specific evidence gap meaning, lexical, question-form index (§8)
Calculate an allowed operation on cited values code
Compose and assess produce and check the answer Haiku + code checks

5.3 Compiling and running

  • Parameterised templates for the common families (pronoun: resolve → passage bundle → compose; doctrine: resolve → retrieve positions → follow links → compose; history: positions → chronology → compare → search gaps → compose).
  • A model proposes a modification only when a template is insufficient; code validates operation types, dependencies, permissions and budgets, else the template runs. Independent operations run concurrently (the LLMCompiler principle, §16).
  • Coverage points per family (what a complete answer establishes) drive at most two re-planning rounds.
  • Success criterion: fewer unnecessary operations and tokens with equal or better completeness — not a valid-looking plan.
  • The run is an EP-Plan execution trace: frame, plan, step activities, outputs, checks.

6. Claim types and their validation (rev 2)

Claim type Required support Code checks
"This passage says…" the passage the span resolves; quotation exact
"This author teaches…" the author's text, attributed the cited item is by that author (not merely about them); stance endorses
"This tradition interprets…" an attributable statement of that tradition the item is a tradition statement or held confession of that tradition
"Taken together, these suggest…" a stated connection and its passages at least two cited spans; the connection is written out; labelled as synthesis
"These figures total…" cited inputs and a reproducible calculation code recomputes
"No earlier example was found" a defined search scope and its recorded results the scope and result count are in the trace
  • The current rule refusing any "says" claim supported by a non-Scripture item is too broad: what Augustine says is supported by Augustine, with its layer explicit. The rule becomes per type.
  • Defeat conditions drive targeted checks: a conclusion resting on a disputed date or on two writings assumed independent is checked for exactly that.
  • The aim is to reduce hedging and unsupported confidence together: the model has a legitimate way to say what follows from the evidence, with the type and limits of that conclusion visible.

7. Perspective, role and purpose (rev 2)

  • Separate three things: reader role (pastor, academic, learner — presentation needs), theological perspective (a tradition), question purpose (study, teaching, devotion, apologetics).
  • The rule, replacing "commit to the reading most Christians hold": a named source or tradition determines the perspective being explained; a user-selected perspective is stated when it matters; an unspecified, disputed question receives a direct explanation of the relevant positions; broad agreement is described only where the evidence supports that description.
  • This lets an answer be confident without treating the mostly Reformed evaluation references as a universal standard.

8. A question-form index and contextual retrieval (rev 2)

  • During ingestion, generate a few candidate questions per passage or reviewed proposition (what it answers, what position it explains, what comparison it serves, how a reader might phrase it) and index them beside the source — document expansion by query prediction (§16).
  • Add concise source context to each indexed unit before embedding and lexical indexing: author, work, section, speaker, subject, and whether the passage reports or rejects a position — contextual retrieval (§16).
  • These are search aids; they are never evidence and never introduce teachings absent from the source.
  • Test on a limited collection, measuring additional useful evidence on unseen questions, before expanding to 3.9M units.
  • Channel decisions by ablation: measure each channel's marginal contribution when removed; a channel that supplies 2% of citations may supply the decisive ones.

9. Skills as executable specifications

Part Example
Applicability the families and aspects it fits
Answer requirements what a satisfactory answer must establish (coverage points)
Plan template the operations that usually supply that evidence
Output structure a direct-answer field, then explanation, then necessary qualifications
Examples good answers and instructive failure cases, from sources outside every evaluation set
Tests cases that detect omission, overstatement and wrong attribution
Operational limits context budget, permitted repairs, latency expectation
  • Packaged as ase:SkillPackage (the agent-skills-engineering context already models packages, cases and assertions); versioned; compiled into the agent; refused by preflight when a part does not check.
  • (rev 2) Improved offline from execution traces and natural-language feedback (GEPA, §16), with the evidence policy, source permissions and release rules frozen; the optimiser may change permitted instructions and examples only; results are tested on held-out questions.
  • (rev 2) No keyword-based opening-sentence gate: repeating the question's words does not show it was answered. The direct-answer field is assessed for supplying the requested information.

10. Our own models, for bounded jobs (rev 2)

Job Training input → output Why first / later
Offline evidence extraction passage → attributed propositions, qualifications, source spans first: improves the knowledge every answer draws on; uncertain outputs reviewed before runtime
Plan selection question + context → template and parameters (validated) small, measurable
Passage relevance question → useful passages, including disagreement replaces or complements the stock reranker
Ask composition question + sufficient evidence → complete, concise, attributed answer later: train against the new evidence representation; include incomplete packets, conflicts, false premises, legitimate synthesis

Training targets are reviewed for completeness as well as support — not simply answers that passed the current checker and judge, which may carry their omissions, padding or confessional assumptions. Each job has one capability objective; "learn all our writings" is too broad to measure.

11. Ontology extensions and the extraction run

11.1 What to add (after exposing what exists)

  • Doctrine: sdo:SupportLink, sdo:Argument, sdo:Qualification, sdo:stance; sdo:Locus (C-box, ~40 loci); propositions a teaching answer needs per locus (~600); tradition statements linked to the held confessional unit that states them.
  • Precepts: sdo:Precept with sdo:Duty / sdo:Prohibition items, supports and fulfilment; grounded on dul:Norm and scr:Command.
  • Concepts: teaching facets for the 184 scr:TheologicalConcepts (today rendering rules): gloss, key passages with role, related, distinctions, common misreadings.
  • Cross-references: the 17,639 OpenBible pairs already in scripture.crossrefs.ttl projected to the runtime store; the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge ingested; relations typed (quotes, alludes, parallels, explains, fulfils, contrasts).
  • Questions as data: frame vocabulary, coverage points, exemplars and the question-form index's generated questions (marked as search aids).
  • Data quality: a date audit of the writings index (e.g. "Against the Arians c. AD 175–180", "the Diatessaron 1st century BC – AD 70").

11.2 The extraction run

Proposed by a model (Haiku, or our extraction adapter, §10), admitted by code, reviewed in the order records most affect answers. Admission rules: every span and verse resolves; an attributed position cites a unit by that author or tradition; a tradition statement paraphrases a held unit (similarity check); no record derived from a source quarantined for the track it would be evaluated on; no 6-word run of a held English Bible edition in generated text. Every record carries prov:wasDerivedFrom, prov:wasGeneratedBy and a review status.

12. Speed and reliability now (rev 2)

  • Prevent repair work. 44% of answers revise, at ~23 s each (~10 s on average). Validate source ids, date eligibility, quotation spans and required fields before drafting; repair the affected claim or section instead of regenerating the answer.
  • Cache verified, immutable evidence bundles by source and annotation version, keeping request-time access checks.
  • Batch passage reads and control concurrency at the data worker; more retries alone do not solve overload.
  • One composition call after deterministic retrieval for common, well-resolved questions; a planning call only when needed. A 10–20 s routine-answer target is to be tested, not assumed.
  • Stream checked sections; progress messages alone do not make an answer faster.

13. Evaluation: two tracks (rev 2)

Track What it tests Source policy
Source-grounded explanation can Ask accurately explain a named source it may retrieve ("what does the Heidelberg Catechism teach about…") the named source is allowed — retrieving it is correct
Independent synthesis can Ask answer from other evidence the reference answer's source and its derivatives are excluded (today 12 of 28 study-note answers cited their own reference note)
  • Systems are compared on the same completed question ids, never one system's partial set against another's full average.
  • The Haiku judge is kept for continuity and calibrated against blinded human review.
  • The model comparison showed that changing the model alone did not solve the problem; it does not prove model capability is irrelevant.
  • Each change is one tag, run from a frozen worktree of its commit; ablations name the component.

14. The pilot and the phases (rev 2)

One complete pilot before expanding the ontology or training broadly:

Order Deliverable Evidence of success
1 A reviewed evaluation slice with explicit source and tradition rules, both tracks trustworthy, paired comparisons
2 Queryable doctrine and interpretation records and reusable evidence bundles previously missing central points reach the composer
3 Typed plans for doctrine, passage explanation and factual questions fewer calls and irrelevant sources
4 Claim types supporting attribution, synthesis and calculation more complete answers without weaker support
5 Question-form indexing and contextual retrieval additional relevant evidence on unseen questions
6 Offline skill optimisation and targeted model training gains persist on held-out families and under load

The first major experiment combines 2–4: give the existing Haiku composer a compact, attributed explanation with its supporting passages, assembled by a small validated plan — directly testing whether the missing knowledge connections and the uniform workflow cause the largest remaining quality gap.

Release gates (independent-synthesis track, same question ids, blind judge calibrated): addresses the question ≥ 0.88, covers the reference ≥ 0.65, restraint ≥ 0.92, Scripture support ≥ 0.90, strict good answer ≥ 0.40 — each above Apologist and Bonfire; confirmed on the 477-question suite.

15. Risks and open questions

  • Prepared doctrine as authority. A model-extracted proposition must never read as what Scripture says; records keep stance, attribution and review status, and answers cite the spans.
  • Extraction quality matters more than volume; review order follows answer impact.
  • Over-planning is bounded by budgets, type checks and template fallback.
  • Optimiser overfitting to the judge: held-out questions and frozen policies guard it; human review samples answers.
  • Open: which perspective leads when a reader sets none and a question is disputed; whether bundles and propositions also serve the Seed reader's Discover panel.

16. Precedents

Research precedents, not predicted gains for this system: Dense X Retrieval (propositions as retrieval units); PropRAG (paths through context-rich propositions, no generative calls online); HippoRAG 2 (passage retrieval integrated with knowledge relationships); LLMCompiler (planning separated from execution, concurrent operations); document expansion by query prediction (doc2query); Anthropic's contextual retrieval (context added before embedding and lexical indexing); GEPA (prompt evolution from traces and natural-language feedback).
