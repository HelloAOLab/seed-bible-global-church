ask-writings v3: know, then prove

Specification, for review. Revision 4, 1 November 2026. Status: proposed, on a measurement made today. Revisions 2 and 3 improved retrieval. Before building either, we ran the control that had never been run: Haiku 5.5 alone, one call, no retrieval, no evidence rule, on the same 80-question development slice, under the same blind judge. It beat our harness by a wide margin and beat both reference apps. The harness, as shaped today, makes the model worse. That measurement, not a paper, decides the design: draft what the model knows, then prove every claim against the signed Scripture corpus and the held writings, strike or soften what cannot be proved, and add what only our library can add. Retrieval-first survives where the model's knowledge is unreliable. The proposition graph of revision 3 becomes the proof substrate. Revision 4b (the same evening) adds the owner's decision to bring the doctrinal sources in — until now held out in favour of Scripture and the writings of its period — and the evidence policy that says, as ontology data, when an ask may draw on each layer.

2. Why the harness loses

  • The missing source, first. Asked WSC 18 ("wherein consists the sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell?"), Haiku, Apologist and Bonfire all state Westminster's formulation — the guilt of Adam's first sin, the want of original righteousness, the corruption of the whole nature (the three together "original sin"), and the actual transgressions that proceed from it. Our answer is a careful Genesis 3 study that never states it. We do not hold the Westminster, Heidelberg or 1689 catechisms (the library has the Racovian), so nothing was retrieved to say it, and the evidence rule forbade the model to say it unsourced. This is a source-coverage and answer-policy failure before it is a retrieval failure: "Westminster teaches X" is proved by the catechism itself; only "these passages support X" needs the interpretive argument. The harness must be able to establish the first without reconstructing the doctrine from Scripture. (Not "the formulation every catechism gives": Heidelberg explains the fall differently, and Catholic teaching draws its own distinctions between inherited sin, personal fault and the condition of nature. Related formulations, with real differences — the answer names whose it is.)
  • Retrieval helps on rare facts and can hurt on well-known ones. Mallen et al. (2022) and Maekawa et al. (2024) measured this for factual QA: for facts the model knows well, retrieved context displaces correct knowledge with partial or distracting passages. They do not show that Haiku knows catechism formulations; the bare run does (claims-covered 0.73 on the catechism family, one repeat), and whether retrieval itself harms those answers is what the 2×2 experiment in §10 measures.
  • The pool is not the argument. ~500 passages from fifteen channels reach a composer that must reconstruct the teaching. The model's own compressed knowledge of the teaching is better organised than the pool.
  • Our strengths are real and narrow: citation verification against a signed corpus, layer separation, exact quotations, the period writings, the original languages, the story layer. None of them requires evidence-first composition; all of them can be applied to a draft.

3. The design: know, then prove

One harness, one shape for every question. The model goes first; the library proves, corrects and enriches.

Step What happens Who Budget
1 Frame family, anchors (verses, lemmas, entities, concepts — resolved to IRIs), lens (§4.2), answer form; the question only, no catalog small call or our plan model + code < 1 s
2 Draft the answer the model knows, direct answer first, the lens's rules applied, references to Scripture by chapter and verse, each claim marked with its kind (§5.3) Haiku 5.5, one call 3–5 s
3 Decompose the draft split into claims (self-contained propositions) with their stated support; typed: Scripture says · X teaches · tradition T holds · broadly held · word means · synthesis · fact/number code + our decomposer model < 1 s
4 Prove each claim checked against the substrate (§5) in parallel: a Scripture claim → the verse resolved in the signed corpus and its text in the reader's edition replaces any quotation from memory; a teaching claim → the proposition graph and held confessions; a tradition claim → that tradition's profile; a fact → KB/chronology; a sense claim → lemma usage; entailment judged by our verifier code + verifier model 3–6 s, parallel
5 Decide each claim: proved (cited) · held by a named source (attributed) · unproved. Unproved claims are softened ("some hold…") or struck by rule, never silently kept; a struck claim that was central triggers one targeted retrieval for that slot code —
6 Enrich only when the frame asks for it (§6): period writings, the original-language note, the story-layer referent, the history of reading — sourced paragraphs from today's retrieval, bounded retrieval + Haiku 0–10 s
7 Render the draft edited minimally (RARR's principle: preserve the draft; change only what proof requires); verified citations replace memory ones; each claim's status is carried in the payload for the reader's UI Haiku, one short call, or code when only citations changed 2–4 s

Target: 10–15 s for a common question, against 60–120 s today; cost ≈ $0.003 (the draft, the verifier, a short render), against ≈ $0.03.

4. Routing: when to trust the model

4.1 Draft-first or retrieve-first

Family Shape Why
Doctrine, catechism, "what does the Bible teach about" draft-first well-known; the model's formulation beat every retrieval arm
Explain this verse / passage draft-first + enrich (context, cross-references, story referents) bare 0.46 vs ours 0.22 on study notes
Who is "he" / "they" here deterministic: story layer the answer is a record
Word study draft-first, proved by lemma usage (code) counts beat recollection
What does Josephus / Augustine / the Didache say retrieve-first in that work rare and attributable; the model paraphrases from memory and mis-cites
Period context, dates, manuscripts, history of reading retrieve-first (chronology, writings, DSS) the model invents dates; we hold the records
Factual (who, when, how many) KB/chronology query + calculate; the model only phrases exact
Pastoral, apologetic draft-first; Scripture claims proved; restraint rules the question is the person's

A popularity proxy refines the route inside a family (Mallen's adaptive retrieval): an anchor with many holdings and many independent assertions in the graph is well-known → draft-first; a rare entity, work or verse → retrieve-first. The proxy is computed from the graph, logged on every run, and tuned on the slice.

4.2 Lenses

Reader role, theological perspective and purpose are separate. A named source or tradition sets the perspective and the answer explains it as theirs; a reader-chosen perspective is stated; a question with none named is settled when independent traditions in the graph agree and disputed when they conflict — a computed fact, which replaces the current "reading most Christians hold" rule. The draft step receives the lens as instructions; the prove step checks "broadly held" against the graph.

4b. The evidence policy: tiers × standpoint × kind

Revision 5 (1 Nov 2026, decided). The owner's rule: a tradition's "terms and thinking" belong in an answer only when the ask is made under that tradition — and the words for it should be the ones theologians and church leaders use, not ours. "Denomination" was the wrong word: a denomination (PCA, SBC, ELCA) is an institution that adopts a standard, never a source of teaching. The gate is now stated in three terms, and each is decided by code or by data, not by the model's taste.

The tiers of teaching — by who holds it

This is the distinction the church has always drawn (dogma · doctrine · opinion; today's "theological triage"), cut by who holds a teaching — which the doctrine graph's adoption assertions record, so the tier of a formulation is derived, never typed.

Tier What it is Where it may appear How it is said
scripture the text and its own period: Scripture, versions, witnesses, the period writings the ground of every answer cited and, where quoted, in the reader's edition's words
ecumenical what the undivided church confessed: the ecumenical creeds and the first councils' definitions (Apostles', Nicene-Constantinopolitan, Chalcedon, Athanasian) — a formulation adopted across every tradition every standpoint, including none attributed to the creed: "the Nicene Creed confesses…"
confessional a tradition's standard (catechism, confession, articles), its characteristic positions and its technical vocabulary — adopted by one tradition or some only inside that tradition's standpoint, or where a comparison asks for the traditions attributed to the standard: "Westminster teaches…"; never as what Scripture says
theologian a named teacher's view (Augustine, Aquinas, Calvin, Wesley) where the question kind allows a later reader as that person's, dated by century
interpretation the readings of a passage: the interpretation layer's questions and paths passage, word-study and history questions as alternatives, with this edition's decision named — never as the plain sense

The standpoint of an ask

Standpoint is the tradition an ask is made from. It is read in this order and nothing else opens the confessional tier: (1) the reader's chosen tradition — the UX setting Tradition: none · Reformed · Lutheran · Anglican · Baptist · Anabaptist · Wesleyan/Methodist · Pentecostal · Roman Catholic · Eastern Orthodox, where none means Scripture and the early church and is the default; (2) the tradition the question itself names or whose own practice it asks about ("why is a saint's name given at baptism" is a Roman Catholic practice and is answered as the Roman Catholic Church explains it); (3) the tradition of a standard the question names (a Westminster question is Reformed). With no standpoint, the answer names no tradition, standard or school and uses none of their technical terms; a biblical word a tradition also uses (justification, covenant, election, grace) is Scripture's and stays, and Trinity and incarnation are the creeds' and stay.

What the reader sees. Every answer says its standpoint in one line ("Reading from: Reformed" / "No tradition chosen — Scripture and the early church"), and each paragraph carries the tier of what it rests on: Scripture · the creeds · Reformed standard (Westminster) · Augustine's view · a disputed reading. Changing the tradition re-asks; it never silently re-colours an answer already given.

How it is enforced. The prompt states the tiers and the standpoint. After the draft, code lists every confessional-tier term that appears with no standpoint (a term list by tradition — the seed is in code, the ontology will carry it as sdo: tradition terms); the slice counts those escapes before a revise call is spent on them. The policy below is read from the ontology's sdo:AskEvidencePolicy rows, one per kind × tier.

The layers the first cut named, with WSC 18 as the worked example, for the record (the ontology terms are unchanged; only the gate's vocabulary moved to the tiers above):

Layer WSC 18 Ontology
Scripture and its period witnesses Gen 3; Rom 5:12–19; Eph 2:3; Ps 51:5 — the LXX, the scrolls, Philo on the fall scr:, txt:, the writings domain's period layer
Doctrinal topic original sin and humanity's fallen condition sdo:Locus (new C-box), scr:TheologicalConcept
Doctrinal formulation the particular explanation WSC 18 gives sdo:DoctrinalFormulation (exists) with a stable id per question/article

4b.1 The frame's fields

  • source: a work or formulation the question names ("WSC 18", "the Heidelberg", "Augustine on…"). An explicit source in the question takes precedence over everything else.
  • tradition: a tradition the question names ("what do Catholics hold", "the Reformed view").
  • perspective → the reader's chosen tradition (context.perspective in the ask context, beside role; the UX calls it Tradition); the default is none, which is Scripture-and-period. Together with source and tradition it yields the standpoint (above), which is what the gate reads.
  • kind decides the answer the question requests: explain a named formulation ("explain WSC 18") → that source's teaching first; doctrinal overview ("what is original sin?") → the topic across traditions, naming the significant differences; earliest teaching ("what did the first Christians teach about…") → dated period and early witnesses only; passage ("what does Romans 5:12 mean") → Scripture and period, doctrine only if the reader's perspective asks for it.

4b.2 The policy, as data

One record per question kind in the doctrine data graph — sdo:AskEvidencePolicy (⊑ at:Norm; the same shape as sdo:PolicyUse already used by translation decisions): for each layer, required · allowed · excluded, and the order of presentation. The frame step reads it; the prove step enforces it; preflight holds every kind to one policy. As loaded (revision 5): the rows are keyed kind × tier (scripture · ecumenical · confessional · theologian · interpretation), the rule allowed-under-perspective is renamed allowed-under-standpoint, and the first cut's columns below read as: formulation + tradition profiles → confessional, later readers → theologian; ecumenical is allowed in every kind but factual, after Scripture; interpretation is allowed for passage, word-study and history questions. Under the owner's rule confessional is allowed-under-standpoint for every kind except explain a named formulation (required — the named standard is the standpoint) and compare (required — the question names the traditions).

Question kind Scripture + period Formulation / source Tradition profiles Later readers Order
Explain a named formulation required (its proof texts) required: the named one allowed: its own allowed if asked teaching → its terms → Scripture support → differences
Doctrinal overview required required: one per major tradition held required, named allowed direct answer → formulations named → Scripture → differences
What does the Bible teach about X required allowed, attributed, after Scripture allowed, named allowed Scripture → where traditions agree/differ
Explain a passage required excluded unless perspective set excluded unless set allowed, as theirs meaning → context → readers
Earliest teaching / history of reading required allowed, dated allowed, dated required, by date earliest first
Who / where / when (factual) required excluded excluded excluded record → verse
Pastoral required allowed only under perspective same allowed Scripture → counsel

Two rules hold across the table: a formulation is always attributed ("Westminster teaches…"), never stated as what Scripture says; and a neutral reading with no source, tradition or perspective stays on Scripture and its period, reporting traditions only where the question kind requires them. That keeps the line the owner drew while letting a Westminster question get a Westminster answer.

5. The proof substrate

Proving needs a store that answers "is this claim what this source says, and who holds it?" faster and more exactly than passage search. That is the proposition graph of revision 3, now justified by a measured need and built in the order the prove step needs it.

5.1 What a claim is proved against

Claim type Proved against Code decides
Scripture says the signed corpus; the reader's edition the reference resolves; the verse's text (not a memory quote) is shown; the verifier judges that the verse states or implies the claim
X teaches X's assertions in the graph; X's held works an assertion by X with stance holds; otherwise "unproved" even if the claim is true
Tradition T holds T's profile (confessions, catechisms, representative authors) profile membership; date within range
Broadly held the consensus lens ≥ 3 independent profiles, dependsOn respected
The word means lemma + usage across Scripture, LXX, period writings occurrence counts recomputed; a sense claim with no usage is softened
Taken together ≥ 2 proved claims and a stated connection labelled synthesis
Fact / number / date KB, chronology recomputed; a date the records do not carry is struck

5.2 The graph (from revision 3, condensed)

  • Nodes: proposition (sdo:Proposition, decontextualised), assertion (speaker, stance holds · reports · rejects · qualifies, span, date), argument (sdo:Argument, scheme), span (txt:Span), verse and token, lemma (scr:KeyTerm), entity (KB), concept, interpretation question, agent and tradition profile.
  • Edges: about (verse · lemma · entity · concept), supports · attacks · qualifies, paraphrases/sameAs across sources, quotes · alludes · parallels (OpenBible + TSK + scroll quotations), dependsOn between sources, defeatedBy.
  • The original-language spine: a writing's words aligned to the verse's tokens (citation, quotation matching at 0.989 precision on the scrolls, cross-lingual alignment), so a Greek homily, a Hebrew midrash and an English note on one lemma meet at one node. Today 25,397 original-language texts are in the index and about 10% of gathered passages are in an original language, read as opaque text; nothing joins them to the lemma.
  • Built offline on RunPod: segment → propositionise → attribute → anchor → relate → admit by code (span resolves, anchor is an IRI, no date the source lacks, no six-word run of a held English Bible, licence allows). Haiku labels a few thousand units; our models do the rest.
  • Stored in GraphDB as the authority and projected to a new D1 database + Vectorize for the runtime (GraphDB Free is never queried per question).
  • Read in waves: wave 0 projects what exists (162 doctrine propositions, 231 commitments, 65 chains, 2,861 interpretation claims, 17,639 cross-references, the story layer); wave 1 the confessions and catechisms, the Fathers' commentaries, the period writings; wave 2 the rabbinic commentary and Targums; wave 3 the long tail by measured demand — a claim the prove step could not settle is logged with its anchors and chooses what is read next.
  • Quarantine: a source that is a reference answer in an evaluation set is extracted into its own named graph, never loaded on the independent-synthesis track.

5.3 Claim marks in the draft

The draft prompt asks the model to mark each claim's kind inline (a light syntax the decomposer reads: [S Rom 5:12] a Scripture claim, [A Augustine] an author claim, [T Reformed] a tradition claim, [F] a fact, [Y] a synthesis). Measured on the slice first: if marking lowers the draft's quality, the decomposer infers kinds instead.

6. What only we can add

Enrichment is where the library earns its place in the answer, bounded and only when the frame calls for it:

  • The original languages: the lemma, its gloss, the usage count, one or two period uses — a sentence, proved by code.
  • The story layer: who is on stage, who "he" is, where and when — a record, not a search.
  • The period writings: what the Septuagint, the scrolls, Philo, Josephus or the Targums do with this verse, as theirs, dated by the chronology.
  • The history of reading: first attestation, the traditions' positions, ordered by date, dependence respected.
  • The reader's edition: every verse shown in the edition the reader chose, with its commitment — the thing no other Ask app does at all.

Each enrichment is a sourced paragraph with its own budget; the direct answer never waits for it, and the answer streams the proved core first.

7. Holdings

  • A small, authoritative doctrinal collection first (the owner's decision, 1 November): the Westminster Shorter and Larger Catechisms and Confession, the Heidelberg Catechism and the 1689 Baptist Confession, from their public-domain editions, stored as complete questions/answers or articles with stable identifiers (wsc.18, wcf.6.4, heidelberg.7), edition and source attribution, confessions distinguished from catechisms. Then across traditions as the product requires: Augsburg and Luther's Small Catechism, the Thirty-Nine Articles, the Belgic Confession and the Canons of Dort, the Catechism of Trent and Baltimore. Each is published as a writing (so Ask quotes it with a commitment) and projected as sdo:DoctrinalFormulations under its tradition's sdo:DoctrinalProfile; the denominations adopting it are sdo:AdoptionAssertions. Those that are evaluation gold are also extracted into the quarantine graph (a named-source question is open-book by policy; a synthesis question is not).
  • If a named source is not held, the answer reports the verification gap ("the catechism is not held here; the formulation is stated from the model and not verified") or retrieves it through an approved external path — never a silent unverified quotation.
  • Keep the detailed-English-search rule: a held edition is a publishable, rights-stated one.
  • Fix the writings index dates the audit found wrong ("Against the Arians c. AD 175–180") before any date is used as proof.

8. Skills and our own models

  • Skills become draft-and-prove specifications (ase:SkillPackage): the family's lens rules, the draft prompt, the required slots (a direct answer, Scripture support, the lens's positions), the enrichments allowed, examples including failures, tests. Improved offline from traces (GEPA) with evidence and permission rules frozen; no keyword gate on the opening sentence — a direct-answer field, assessed.
  • Our models, each a bounded job with a held-out test, on RunPod: the verifier (does this span state or imply this claim? — the central model of the design, trained on Haiku-labelled and reviewer-checked pairs, including incomplete, conflicting and false-premise cases), the claim decomposer, the propositioniser and attributor for the offline build, the cross-lingual aligner, a reranker over propositions. Haiku 5.5 stays the drafter and renderer. The 9B composer experiment is retired: composition is not where the gap was.

9. Evaluation: the bare model is the baseline

  • Every harness change must beat haiku-bare on the same question ids, three repeats, paired. Beating Apologist and Bonfire is necessary, not sufficient: a harness that scores below its own model is subtracting value.
  • Two tracks: source-grounded (the named source may be retrieved) and independent synthesis (the reference source's graph is not loaded). The bare model is a third reading: what the model knows without us.
  • Code checks replace the judge where code can decide: every reference resolves in the corpus (done today for the four arms); every quotation of Scripture matches the reader's edition (the harness guarantees it; the bare model cannot — this is the proof step's first measurable win); every date is carried by a record; every author claim has an assertion by that author.
  • Proposition-level coverage: reference answers decomposed the same way as drafts; coverage = matched propositions; auditable.
  • The judge is calibrated against blinded human review before any number is quoted; judge and revise shape are named beside every number.

10. Build order and kill criteria

Step Deliverable Gate
0 haiku-bare × 3 repeats on the slice; the quotation and reference code checks on all arms; every slice item labelled with its intended source or perspective (a Westminster question is graded against Westminster; a general question must not need an unattributed Westminster answer to pass) the baseline and its variance; source-fidelity graded apart from coverage
0b The 2×2: {current sources, + the doctrinal collection} × {current harness, draft-then-prove}, model and questions fixed, named-catechism items open-book, synthesis items apart how much comes from the missing sources and how much from the shape — decided before either is generalised
1 Draft → prove Scripture claims → render: the draft, references resolved in the signed corpus, quotations replaced by the reader's edition, unproved Scripture claims softened; nothing else ≥ bare on every judge measure, 100% verified references and quotations, < 15 s
2 Holdings: confessions and catechisms ingested (quarantined where they are gold); tradition profiles; the lens rules in the draft prompt catechism family ≥ 0.65 good on the independent track
3 Claim decomposition and typed proof for author · tradition · broadly-held · fact claims over wave 0 + wave 1 of the graph; the verifier model restraint ≥ 0.92 with claims-covered not below bare; verifier agreement with reviewers ≥ 0.9
4 Routing by family and popularity proxy; retrieve-first families on today's retrieval; enrichment slots; streaming retrieve-first families ≥ bare on their own ids; median time ≤ 15 s
5 The original-language spine; lemma proof for sense claims; waves 2–3 by demand; offline skill optimisation; the 477-question suite against Apologist, Bonfire and bare release gate below

Kill criteria. If step 1 does not reach the bare model's scores, the loss is in the render step and is fixed there before anything else is built. If the verifier's agreement with reviewers is below 0.85, proof falls back to code-only checks (references, quotations, dates) and the graph build pauses. If the popularity proxy does not separate the families where retrieval helps from where it hurts, routing is by family alone.

Release gate (independent track, same ids, calibrated judge): above bare on every measure; addresses ≥ 0.90, covers ≥ 0.70, restraint ≥ 0.92, verified Scripture support 1.00 by code, strict good ≥ 0.55.

11. Risks

  • Confident error from the draft. The model's knowledge includes mistakes it states fluently. Proof covers Scripture, author, tradition, fact and sense claims; syntheses are labelled; what is left unproved is softened. The residual risk is measured as restraint, and it is already lower for bare Haiku (0.85) than for Apologist (0.92) — step 3 exists to close that.
  • Memory quotations of Scripture. The draft quotes from recollection in some edition; step 1 replaces every quotation with the reader's edition, so no memory wording reaches the reader.
  • Tradition bias in "broadly held". Computed only from independent profiles the library actually holds; holdings per tradition are reported.
  • Misattribution in the graph. Stance precision is measured first and gates the build.
  • Losing what the harness does well. Step 1's gate includes the code checks the current harness passes (verified references, exact quotations, layer separation); a regression there fails the step.

12. Precedents

  • Mallen et al. 2022 (PopQA) and Maekawa et al. 2024 (WiTQA): retrieval helps on rare facts and can hurt on well-known ones; adaptive retrieval by popularity improves QA by up to 10 points.
  • RARR (Gao et al. 2023) and ARE (2024): generate from the model, then research and revise — attribute each claim post hoc, edit minimally, preserve the draft; ARE decomposes long answers into units for precise editing.
  • Dense X Retrieval (EMNLP 2024) and PropRAG (EMNLP 2025): propositions as retrieval units; offline proposition graphs with LLM-free path search — the proof substrate's shape.
  • HippoRAG 2, GraphRAG: graph-integrated retrieval and community summaries, for the history-of-reading family.
  • "Do We Still Need GraphRAG?" and GraphRAG-Bench (2025–26): graph retrieval's gains on multi-hop reasoning are modest (≈ 4.5 points on HotpotQA) at 2.3× latency — the reason the graph is built for proof first, not for retrieval.
  • PaperTrail (2026): a claim-evidence interface for scholarly QA — atomic claims extracted offline, evidence retrieved per claim, provenance shown to the reader.
  • GEPA, doc2query, contextual retrieval, LLMCompiler, competency questions, nanopublications and the Argument Interchange Format, as in revisions 2–3.
