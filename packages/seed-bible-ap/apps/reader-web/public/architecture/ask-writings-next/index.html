ask-writings: measured, built, next

For external review. Date: 1 November 2026. Status: partly built, not deployed. This page follows the ask-writings v2 design (31 October, proposed). It reports what two days of measurement found — against our own gold questions, against 477 frozen Christian Ask questions, and against three other ask apps — what was built in response, how the models we trained on RunPod compare with Haiku 5.5, and a detailed plan of next steps, each with the test that decides it.

Review questions

We are asking for a critical read. In order of importance:

  1. Diagnosis (§6). We conclude that the remaining gap to the other apps is in answering what was asked, not in grounding. Do the numbers in §3–§4 support that, or are we over-reading a small slice?
  2. One harness for every question (§2, §8). We will not route ordinary verse or doctrine questions to a separate, simpler ask. Is a single evidence-first harness the right shape for both "what did Josephus add to the flood story" and "what is the chief end of man"?
  3. The next-step order (§9). Is the order right? Is anything missing that would move answer quality more than what we listed?
  4. Trained models (§5, §9.6). Our 9B translates at least as well as Haiku 5.5 but composes answers far worse. Is training a dedicated composer adapter worth it, and what should it be trained on?
  5. Fairness of the comparisons (§4, §10). One judge (Haiku 5.5) grades every system blind; we fixed a defect that hid our citations from it. Is anything else tilting the comparison, either way?

1. Summary

ask-writings is the Scripture Agent's one ask: it gathers evidence from Scripture and the writings around it, plans an answer, composes it from that evidence only, and lets code refuse any claim the evidence does not carry. Two days of measurement changed three things we believed:

  • Evidence packaging, not retrieval, was costing recall. The first "evidence packet" threw away 8 required sources that search had already found — every one of them by a hard per-kind cap. A coverage-first packet keeps them: required recall on our 32 gold questions rose from 0.67 to 0.87 end to end, best of every arm measured.
  • Our answers are better grounded than the other apps' but answer the question less directly. On 80 fixed Christian Ask questions, scored by a blind judge, the improved harness leads on "Scripture actually supports it" (0.88 vs Apologist 0.84, Bonfire 0.54) and trails on "addresses what was asked" (0.68 vs 0.86 and 0.82).
  • Our 9B translates as well as Haiku 5.5 but cannot yet compose answers. Translation: 44.4 vs 43.0 chrF3 on 300 shared rows (207 of 300 rows better). Composition: 0.60 required recall vs 0.81 on identical evidence.

Decision sought: agreement on the order of §9 — finish and confirm the question-first planning, deploy the measured changes, then cut latency, then train a composer adapter on reviewed targets.

4. Against other ask apps

Three apps answered as a guest: Seed Bible's Apologist (gpt-5-mini behind apologist.seedbible.io) and Bonfire AI (bonfire.seedbible.io), and FaithBot (faithbot.donbarger.com; behind a bot check, so its answers were pasted by hand and it is in the writings comparison only).

4.1 Writings questions (27 common questions; one prose grader for every system: a source counts when the answer names it)

System Answered Required recall (prose) Required recall (attached citations) Median time
Ours, v2 harness 27/27 0.580 0.858 115 s
Ours, deployed baseline 26/27 0.593 0.759 131 s
FaithBot 26/27 0.525 — —
Apologist 27/27 0.519 none returned 19 s

The other apps answer from model memory: nothing checks that a citation they name exists or says what they claim. Ours cites held, commitment-verified text. They are six to ten times faster.

4.2 Are FaithBot and Apologist the same engine?

Not as configured: they run on different hosts and APIs, and on the same 26 questions share 0.3% of six-word phrases and 9% of vocabulary (FaithBot's median answer is 400 characters, Apologist's 4,260). They could still sit on the same base model; that cannot be told from outside.

6. What the measurements found

  1. The packet cut what search found. Hard per-kind caps (8 undated items, 25 period items) dropped required sources while the packet sat at 5–8k of a 40k-token budget; six of the eight were passages the question itself named. The first builder also stopped at the first item that overflowed the budget.
  2. Search misses were routing, not holdings. Every required source no run retrieved is held. Causes: a range kept only its first six verses (Genesis 5:27 lost from 5:21–27); a range across chapters was refused; "City of God XV.11" did not match the stored locator "City of God, Book XV, Chapter 11"; the planner named no passage for a work the question implied.
  3. The search index is healthy; the probes were too strict. Every probe passage has a vector, filters work, and a passage queried by its own vector returns first. Exact scoring helps slightly (11 vs 9 of 60). Many probe questions are generic, so "find this one passage" undercounts good retrieval.
  4. The data worker fails under load. At six concurrent asks, two of three passage reads failed and 72 of 108 answers were written without a passage they needed. The agent gave up after three tries in under a second.
  5. The judge never saw our citations. Writings answers carry no "commitment verified" flag on their verses and their cited writings were never shown, so the blind judge graded every one as citing nothing. With the fix, one smoke question's restraint score went from 0.0 to 1.0.
  6. Most questions had no fitting kind. "What does this verse mean", a catechism question and a pastoral question were forced into "what the text says" or "history of reading", whose instructions told the writer to end on what the text does not settle and to fill every layer.
  7. Answers were built around the evidence found, not the question. The remaining gap to the other apps (addresses the question 0.68 vs 0.86) is here.

8. The design as it now stands

Step Who What it does now
classify Haiku 5.5, checked by code The question's kind — now including teach — and its anchors.
gather code About fifteen channels, plus the question channel; waves that do not depend on each other run together; ranges and cited forms resolved by code.
coverage + repair code Checks the pool against the question's obligations; up to two targeted repair searches; the stop reason recorded.
packet (v2) code + Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B Named passages first; ranked within preferences; filled to a hard budget; coverage checked again.
select Haiku 5.5 What the question asks, then an outline around it; every point names evidence ids.
compose Haiku 5.5 Answers each ask in order, names its sources in the prose, lists every claim with its layer and evidence.
check → revise → answer code (revise: Haiku 5.5) Unchanged: code strikes what the evidence does not carry.

9. Next steps, in order

9.1 Confirm question-first planning (now)

Change: already built (§7). Test: re-ask the slice questions where v2b scored "partly" or "no" on addressing the question. Pass: addresses-the-question rises on those questions without restraint or Scripture support falling. Cost: about 15 asks.

9.2 Close the answer-shape gap

  • A direct opening sentence, enforced by code: the check refuses an answer whose first sentence does not address the first ask (a cheap judge-free proxy: it must contain the ask's key terms or a yes/no).
  • Length by question: a short, factual or catechism question gets a short answer; the writings survey is for questions that ask for it.
  • Reference coverage: for doctrine and catechism questions, the outline must cover the standard points of the question (what it is, its scriptural ground, its bearing), so the answer covers what a reference answer covers.

Pass: on the slice, addresses-the-question ≥ 0.80 and covers-the-reference ≥ 0.55 with restraint ≥ 0.85 and Scripture support ≥ 0.85.

9.3 Deploy the measured changes

Packet v2 with Qwen3 (the reranker needs a hosted home: a RunPod serverless endpoint or a small always-on pod), the question channel, the routing fixes, the data-worker retries, the teach kind, question-first planning, and exact vector scoring in the data worker. Gate: the slice shows no measure falling against the deployed agent, and our 32 writings gold questions keep required recall ≥ 0.85.

9.4 Cut latency (about 115 s today; the other apps answer in 12–19 s)

  • Stream the answer as it is written.
  • Merge select into compose for teach questions (one model call instead of two).
  • Prevent the commonest revise causes before drafting (the trace now records them): revise runs on 44% of answers and costs about 23 s.
  • Keep the gather waves parallel (done) and measure the gain.

Pass: median under 60 s, first words on screen under 15 s, no quality measure falling.

9.5 Make the measurement decision-grade

  • Review the 32 writings gold questions case by case (a draft review sheet exists: for each gold item, how often runs cited and retrieved it, and what the runs keep citing instead).
  • Calibrate the judge against the owner's verdicts on about 60 claims before any number is quoted outside the team.
  • Fix a non-inferiority margin before repeats; repeat three times only for a release decision.

9.6 Train a composer adapter (RunPod)

Our 9B composes far worse than Haiku today (§5). A dedicated adapter trained on reviewed question → evidence → claims examples is the path to moving the most expensive step off the metered API. Training data: the harness's own recorded packets and the answers that passed the check and the judge, reviewed; conflicting sources, false premises and "not held" cases included. Serving: schema-constrained decoding (vLLM) so a reply cannot leave the answer format. Gate: on identical packets, within a fixed margin of Haiku on required recall, citation coverage and layer discipline. The design floor from the earlier plan stands: at least 1,000 reviewed targets.

9.7 Use the translation model where it already wins

Offer the 9B as the drafter in the writings translation pipeline beside Haiku 5.5, on the next batch of works, and score it with that pipeline's fidelity checks; widen the dev set beyond 8 source works so the comparison can be decided.

10. How each step is tested

Test Size Grader Used for
Writings gold 32 questions code (cites the gold sources) retrieval and packet changes
Frozen evidence pools 32 pools code packet builders, rankers — no model calls
Christian Ask development slice 80 questions blind Haiku 5.5 judge answer quality; every design step
Christian Ask frozen suite 477 questions blind Haiku 5.5 judge confirming a release
Other ask apps the same questions the same graders where we stand
Identical-packet composer test 32 packets code any model in the compose step

Every answer of ours is graded on what it cites, and every other app's answer on what it says, by the same judge, which is not told which system wrote the answer. The judge is an Anthropic model and two of the compared composers are Anthropic models; the judge's reading of our answers is checked against the owner's verdicts before a number is quoted outside the team.

11. Risks

  • Small slices. 80 questions guide design; they do not prove a release. Confirmation is the full suite.
  • The data worker. Under load it fails; a busy reader hour could produce partial answers until retries and capacity are both in place.
  • Overfitting to the judge. Answer-shape changes could please the judge without helping readers; the owner's review of sampled answers is the check.
  • Speed vs grounding. The other apps are fast because they do not fetch or verify. Cutting latency must not cut verification.
  • Unreviewed gold. The writings gold was accepted in bulk; some "misses" are acceptable alternatives (the Targums, Josephus on Pompey's siege).

12. Open questions

  1. Should "addresses what was asked" be the release gate, ahead of required-source recall?
  2. For doctrine and catechism questions, should the answer state the reference tradition's answer first and then where traditions differ, or treat traditions evenly?
  3. How much latency are readers willing to trade for verified citations — is 30–45 s acceptable if the first words arrive in 10?
  4. Is the Qwen3 reranker worth a hosted service, or should its gain be folded into the data worker's own search?
  5. What should the composer adapter's training set be reviewed for, and by whom?
