Architecture / Ontology / Scripture and the writings

Scripture and the writings: two domains on one shared vocabulary

The system reads the Bible beside many other texts: the Dead Sea Scrolls, Josephus, the Church Fathers, rabbinic commentary, ancient Near Eastern inscriptions, Greek myth. The ontology keeps the Bible apart from every other writing. Each kind of text has its own level, and the rule for deciding where a book goes is written in code. This page sets out the levels, what each one holds, and which books drive each one.

Why the split

Until 27 October 2026 one domain, scripture-writings, held every writing the system cites: the copies of the biblical books, the Apocrypha, the Fathers, councils, rabbinic works and inscriptions. It also imported the Bible ontology. So the classes that describe the Bible and the records of texts that are not the Bible lived in one place. A change made for one kind of text could quietly affect the other. And nothing in the model said which texts are scripture.

The owner's direction was to put all the canonical books in the scripture domain and the non-canonical writings in a separate domain, so that the ontology for Bible content stays separate from the ontology for everything else. The working canon is the 66 books that the project's own translation (RBS) translates. Every other book, including those other churches hold canonical, is a writing. Which traditions hold a writing canonical is still recorded, as data on that writing.

The levels

upper Textual Sources — the vocabulary any text shares

The terms needed to describe any text: the intellectual work, dated by its composition, with a range that spans every dating the sources give; each exact edition or translation, with its own licence and the uses it allows; each material witness (a scroll, a codex, a papyrus), dated by the object; the cited span, whose text is stored only where the edition allows it; the alignment of a span to a passage; and a community's canon classification of a work. It holds no Bible content and no non-Bible content, so a change to it means the same thing for both domains.

domain Scripture — the 66 books and the witnesses that carry their text

Everything about the Bible itself. Its verses and editions, the knowledge base of people and places, the story layer and the interpretation layer were already here. Since the split it also holds the textual records of the canon: the canonical books as works, and every witness whose text is one of them. Those witnesses include the great codices, the early papyri, the ancient translations of the Bible, and the Dead Sea Scrolls that are copies of a biblical book.

domain Writings — every writing that is not one of the 66

Every other text the Bible is read with. That covers the deuterocanonical books and the pseudepigrapha, the Dead Sea Scrolls' own compositions, Jewish writers of the Greek and Roman period, the Church Fathers and councils, rabbinic and Targumic works, inscriptions and literature of the ancient Near East, and Greek and Latin classics. Each carries the communities that hold it canonical, as data. The domain adds two classes of its own:

  • wrt:PublishedWriting: a work as the publishing service serves it, with its address, version and parts. It is recorded only after every part was read back and its commitment recomputed.
  • wrt:ProgrammeTranslation: our own machine-aided English, made from a public-domain original-language edition. Each one records the pipeline and model that made it and its review status (machine draft, model-revised, or reviewed by a person). Only a person can set it to reviewed.

How a record is placed

Code places every record. Nobody sorts them by hand, and a model is never asked.

  1. A work is scripture's when it is one of the 66 books. The test is the whole title: "Psalms" is one of the 66, but "Psalms of Solomon" and "Genesis Rabbah" are not.
  2. A witness is scripture's when its text is one of the 66. These are biblical manuscripts and early versions. Among the Dead Sea Scrolls, a manuscript counts as a copy when its name gives a canonical book (4QSamuel a, MasLev), unless it is a commentary, an apocryphon or a reworking of that book (Pesher Isaiah, the Genesis Apocryphon, the Reworked Pentateuch, 4Q252).
  3. Everything else is the writings domain's. A community's view that a writing is canonical is recorded as a canon classification on that writing. It never moves the writing into scripture.
  4. The writings domain refuses a canonical book. When a published work's title is one of the 66, the build stops and names it.

One judgement call is worth naming: a translation of the Bible counts as a witness of the Bible. That puts the Septuagint, Peshitta and Vulgate, and the Qumran Targum of Job, in scripture. The rabbinic Targums (Onkelos, Jonathan) count as rabbinic literature, so they are writings. Changing that is one line of the placement rule.

How the two stay apart

  • Neither imports the other. Scripture and writings both import textual-sources; neither imports the other's ontology.
  • A link is a reference, not an import. A writing's span reaches a verse through txt:toPassage "Gen.6.4", a string checked against the versification. A writing says which verse it bears on without the writings ontology knowing what a verse is.
  • Separate named graphs. The canon's records and the writings' records are loaded into different graphs. Reloading one never touches the other.
  • Interpretations are scripture's; their evidence may be a writing. A claim about what a passage means lives in scripture's interpretation layer. Where it cites Rashi or Josephus, it names that writing's record by its IRI, the way one names a book in a footnote.

Where it lives

  • ~/skills/ontology/textual-sources.ttl (+ .clusters.ttl, .data.ttl for the controlled values): the upper.
  • ~/skills/ontology/writings.ttl (+ clusters); writings.data.ttl, writings.sa.ttl, writings.published.ttl, writings.dss/: the writings domain and its records.
  • ~/skills/ontology/scripture.ttl; scripture.canon.ttl, scripture.dss/: the canon's records.
  • Generated, never hand-edited: scripts/build-writings-abox.py (from the chronology) places each record, scripts/build-dss-abox.py places each scroll, and scripts/writings/build-published-abox.py builds from the publishing receipt. Each has a --check in preflight.
