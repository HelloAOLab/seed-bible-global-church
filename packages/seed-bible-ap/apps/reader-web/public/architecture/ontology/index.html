Architecture / Ontology

Ontology: what the records mean

Every highlight, note, bookmark and reading plan is saved as a small typed document. The type says what it is in words both people and machines agree on. Seed Bible does not invent those words from scratch: it builds on layers of shared vocabulary, each one resting on the layer below.

The stack PROV-O P-Plan DOLCE+DnS agentic-primitives agentic-trust Faith Scripture Seed Worked example

Short version. A Seed highlight is a seed:Highlight. That is a kind of knowledge record (agentic-trust), which is a kind of entity with a provenance (PROV-O). It points at a passage (scripture) and is held by a person, which is just PROV-O's "was attributed to". So a tool that has never heard of Seed Bible can still tell who made it, from what, and when.

The stack

Read the picture from top to bottom. Each arrow means "builds on". A layer may reach down into any layer below it, never up: the scripture ontology knows nothing about Seed Bible, and the faith ontology knows nothing about scripture's verses. A check in the skills repository (check_domain_firewall) enforces this.

The foundations

Three public, long-standing ontologies sit at the bottom. Every class above them must eventually connect to one of these. The skills repository keeps a trimmed copy of each (only the terms actually used, with the upstream definitions word for word) in ontology/upper/.

foundation PROV-O — provenance

The W3C standard for saying where something came from. It has three core ideas: an Entity (a thing, such as a document), an Activity (something that happened over time), and an Agent (someone responsible). It then links them: this entity was generated by that activity, was attributed to that agent, and so on.

Key terms
prov:Entity, prov:Activity, prov:Agent (Person, Organization, SoftwareAgent), prov:wasAttributedTo, prov:wasGeneratedBy, prov:actedOnBehalfOf
Why Seed needs it
Every record has to say whose it is. seed:heldBy and seed:publishedBy are declared as narrower forms of prov:wasAttributedTo. "The workspace acted on behalf of the reader" is exactly prov:actedOnBehalfOf.
Upstream
w3.org/TR/prov-o

foundation P-Plan and EP-Plan — plans and their runs

P-Plan extends PROV-O with plans: a Plan is made of Steps, and each Step takes and produces Variables. EP-Plan adds the other half: an execution trace that records which step a real activity carried out. Together they let us say both "this is what was supposed to happen" and "this is what did happen".

Key terms
p-plan:Plan, p-plan:Step, p-plan:Variable, p-plan:correspondsToStep, ep-plan:ExecutionTraceBundle
Why Seed needs it
Every write is a harness run on an agent: a small plan (for example "save this file to the Library, then mark it private") that the agent checks and carries out. at:Playbook and at:CoordinationPlan are P-Plan plans; at:ExecutionTrace is an EP-Plan trace bundle.
Upstream
purl.org/net/p-plan · w3id.org/ep-plan

foundation DOLCE+DnS Ultralite — descriptions and situations

DUL is a general-purpose ontology of what kinds of things exist. The part we use most is Descriptions and Situations (DnS): a Description defines roles and rules (a plan, a design, a set of norms), and a Situation is a real state of affairs that satisfies it. It also gives us Roles (something an agent plays rather than is) and Information Objects (content that needs a carrier).

Key terms
dul:Description, dul:Situation, dul:Role, dul:Concept, dul:InformationObject, dul:Collection
Why Seed needs it
A live reading session is a Situation: its options (who may navigate, who may highlight) are the Description it satisfies, and "host" is a Role a person plays. seed:ReadingSession ⊑ at:Situation ⊑ dul:Situation. A passage is a dul:Collection of verses.
Upstream
ontologydesignpatterns.org/ont/dul

The agentic layer

These two layers describe agents, what they may do, and the evidence they leave. They are domain-neutral: the same terms describe a family office, a heart-coaching app, or a Bible reader.

agentic agentic-primitives — the substrate's vocabulary

@agenticprimitives/ontology is the formal vocabulary of the substrate Seed runs on: identity, credentials, custody, delegation, naming, registries, discovery, skills, situations, runs and audit. It is load-bearing: the same terms exist on chain (faithchain's OntologyTermRegistry) and as TypeScript constants, and a check fails if they drift apart. It names and constrains; it never grants anything.

Modules
ap: core, apid: identity, apdel: delegation, apnam: naming, aps: skills, apsit: situations, apexec: execution, apcnt: content, apreg: registry, apdisc: discovery, aptrust: trust
Grounded in
PROV-O and DOLCE+DnS (for example apsit:Situation, apexec:SkillExecution ⊑ prov:Activity)
Why Seed needs it
The wire that lets the workspace ask as you is an apdel: delegation; the names seed-reader.workspace and seedreader.me are apnam: names; the town's index of published works is apcnt:PublishedWork. Seed never adds its own words here (rule ADR-0021).

agentic agentic-trust — the base every domain inherits

at: is the cleaner, smaller re-cut of agentic-primitives that every domain ontology in the skills repository subclasses. It points at the substrate terms with rdfs:subClassOf where one really is a kind of the other, and rdfs:seeAlso where they merely correspond. Its one rule, carried over word for word: evidence is never authority. A record, a plan step or a signed card grants nothing; permission is checked when it is used.

Key terms
at:KnowledgeRecord (any domain record), at:Activity, at:Situation, at:Delegation, at:Category, at:Container, at:Person, at:Organization, at:ChurchAgent, at:WorkspaceAgent, at:ServiceAgent, at:Skill, at:Capability, at:Playbook
Grounded in
at:Agent ⊑ prov:Agent · at:Activity ⊑ prov:Activity · at:Situation ⊑ dul:Situation · at:Category ⊑ skos:Concept, dul:Concept · at:Plan ⊑ p-plan:Plan · at:ExecutionTrace ⊑ ep-plan:ExecutionTraceBundle
Why Seed needs it
Most Seed classes subclass one of five at: classes (see the table below), and every act the reader asks for is an at:Skill on a person, org or workspace agent.

The faith and scripture domains

domain Faith — the church today

The faith ontology is the Global.Church Core Ontology (gc:, with incubator terms under fci:), re-cut onto the agentic-trust base. Everything domain-neutral it used to carry (organizations, persons, membership, offerings, attestation) moved up into at:. What remains is genuinely about the church: ekklesia bodies and gatherings, people groups, ministry activity, and mission engagement.

Key terms
gc:EkklesiaCommunity (the persistent gathered body), gc:AgentiveEkklesia (the body when it acts, also an at:Organization), fci:CongregationMembership
Grounded in
gc:EkklesiaCommunity ⊑ at:Community ⊑ prov:Entity
Why Seed needs it
When a church publishes a reading plan or a branded reader theme, it does so as a .church org agent. The body behind that agent is a gc:AgentiveEkklesia, so a congregation's work can be tied to the congregation, not just to an account.
Upstream
ontology.global.church/core

domain Scripture — the text the church reads

Scripture describes the Bible as a verifiable graph. A Verse is a place in the Bible (John 3:16) that never changes. Its wording in one translation is a separate thing, a VerseText, and each translation's published state is an Edition with a Merkle root, so one verse can be proven to belong to the edition without sending the rest. It also models the narrative: events, people, places and speech acts.

Key terms
scr:Verse, scr:Passage, scr:VerseText, scr:ScriptureEdition, scr:ScriptureCorpus, scr:BiblicalEvent
Grounded in
scr:Verse ⊑ pub:ContentWork · scr:VerseText ⊑ pub:ContentRevision · scr:ScriptureEdition ⊑ pub:CorpusRelease · scr:Passage ⊑ at:Container ⊑ dul:Collection · reaches into faith for scr:ApostolicBody
Why Seed needs it
Seed never models a verse itself (there is no second verse model). Every mark points at a scr:Passage in a named scr:ScriptureEdition. Rule: the text is not here — no Seed class carries Bible wording; the Scripture agent serves it.

Seed — the reader's own words

seed: is the small vertical ontology for reading Scripture together. It lives in the skills repository (ontology/seed.ttl) with SHACL shapes that say which fields each record must have. Almost every class is a narrower form of a class from a layer below:

Seed class Is a kind of Which climbs to In plain words
seed:Highlight, Annotation, Bookmark, Save, ReadingPosition, ReaderSettings, Profile, PlanProgress at:KnowledgeRecord prov:Entity Your private records. Each is held by exactly one person.
seed:ReadingEvent, PlaylistPlay at:Activity prov:Activity Something you did, with a start and an end.
seed:Playlist, ReadingPlan, Offering, Customization pub:ContentWork at:ContentArtifact → prov:Entity Works you or a church can publish, with revisions and releases.
seed:ReadingSession at:Situation dul:Situation, prov:Entity A live shared reading and its rules.
seed:ShareGrant at:Delegation dul:InformationObject Evidence that someone may read a private slice. Never a gate by itself.
seed:HighlightColor, Visibility, SessionRole, OfferingKind at:Category skos:Concept, dul:Concept Controlled lists (world / friends / private, and so on).

The properties tie Seed to the other layers in the same way:

Property Points to Note
seed:aboutPassage scr:Passage What a mark, event or offering is about
seed:ofEdition scr:ScriptureEdition Which translation
seed:heldBy at:Person Narrower form of prov:wasAttributedTo; never the workspace or a service
seed:publishedBy at:Organization or at:Person Narrower form of prov:wasAttributedTo
seed:visibility seed:Visibility Private unless it says otherwise

Five rules the Seed ontology keeps

  1. A mark is the person's. Held by exactly one person, never by the app or a service.
  2. The text is not here. No Seed record carries Bible wording.
  3. A listing is not a grant. Being findable in the town says nothing about who may read.
  4. A secret is a pointer. seed:SecretRef names a vault entry; it never holds the value.
  5. Visibility is explicit. Private unless the record says otherwise. (The old reader made everything public by default.)

Worked example: one highlight

You highlight John 3:16 in yellow. The reader writes this document to your Library under seed-bible/, private by default: 

{
  "@context": { "seed": "https://skills.demo/seed#", "scr": "https://skills.demo/scripture#" },
  "@type": "seed:Highlight",
  "@id": "urn:seed:highlight:9f2c…",
  "seed:aboutPassage": "osis:John.3.16",
  "seed:ofEdition": "BSB",
  "seed:highlightColor": "seed:HighlightColor-yellow",
  "seed:heldBy": "did:…:seedreader.me",
  "seed:visibility": "seed:Visibility-private"
}

Anyone holding the ontology can now reason upward without knowing Seed Bible:

Question Answered by
What kind of thing is it? seed:Highlight → at:KnowledgeRecord → prov:Entity (and oa:Annotation by cross-reference)
Whose is it? seed:heldBy is a prov:wasAttributedTo, pointing at an at:Person → prov:Agent
What is it about? A scr:Passage in a scr:ScriptureEdition whose text can be proven against the edition's root
How did it get there? A harness run on your agent (an at:ExecutionTrace, an EP-Plan trace) carrying out a step of a p-plan:Plan, by the workspace acting on your behalf (prov:actedOnBehalfOf) under a delegation

Where it lives

  • ~/skills/ontology/seed.ttl — the Seed classes and SHACL shapes; seed.data.ttl holds the controlled values and the skills, capabilities and agent archetypes.
  • ~/skills/ontology/{scripture,faith,publishing,commerce}.ttl and agentic-trust/tbox.ttl — the layers below.
  • ~/skills/ontology/upper/{prov-o,ep-plan,dolce,web-annotation}.ttl — the trimmed foundations.
  • @agenticprimitives/ontology (tbox/, cbox/, mappings/) — the substrate vocabulary.
  • All of it is loaded into GraphDB as named graphs (Seed is <urn:skills:context:seed>).
