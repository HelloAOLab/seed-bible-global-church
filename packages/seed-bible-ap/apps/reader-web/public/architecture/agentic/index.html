Architecture / Agentic

Agentic: who talks to whom

The app in your browser talks to only two places: your Home, to sign in, and the reader's workspace agent, for everything else. The workspace then talks to the other agents over A2A (agent-to-agent messages), and asks your own agent to act for you.

The three hops, in plain words

  1. App → Home: sign in. You sign in at your Home, not in the app. The Home gives the app a short-lived token that says who you are, and gives the workspace a wire: a permission to ask your agent to act as you, limited to the reader's tasks. You can see and revoke it at your Home under Connected assistants.
  2. App → workspace: every other call. The app sends one kind of request to seed-reader-a2a.faithnet.io, with your token attached: "please do this reader task" (for example seed.bucket.merge or seed.workspace.friends). Bible reads go to /bible/api/* on the same host. The workspace checks the token against the Home's public keys before it does anything.
  3. Workspace → agents: the actual work. The workspace turns each task into a short plan and sends it to the right agent. Writes go to your agent, which checks the wire and writes into your Library itself. The workspace never writes your data directly and never sees your keys.

Three trips through the map

Opening John 3

  1. The app asks the workspace for /bible/api/BSB/JHN/3.json.
  2. The workspace answers from its edge cache, or sends SendMessage to the Scripture agent and caches the answer.
  3. The app checks the chapter's signature against the edition's root on your device, then shows it. Next time it opens offline from the device.

Highlighting John 3:16

  1. The highlight appears at once: the app keeps it in the browser's own database and queues it.
  2. When you are signed in and online, the app asks the workspace to merge the device's bucket of highlights into your Library (seed.bucket.merge, one request, your token attached).
  3. The workspace combines the device's copy with the one already in your Library using the reader tools (so neither side loses a mark), then sends your agent a short plan ("save this file of seed:Highlight records to the Library, private") under your wire.
  4. Your agent checks the wire is live and the step is one the reader may ask for, then writes the record into your Library. The record is yours; the workspace keeps no copy that counts.

Seeing a friend's note in Today

  1. Your friend published a note: their agent marked it public and signed a release on their shelf.
  2. Within 15 minutes the serving plane's crawl finds it, checks the release, and indexes it by chapter.
  3. Today asks the workspace for your friends (from your Home's contacts) and what they published; the workspace asks the serving plane.
  4. When you open the note, its body comes from the public door, straight from your friend's shelf.

What the browser never does

  • It never calls the Scripture agent, the serving plane, the tool server or anyone's agent directly.
  • It never holds an API key or a signing key. The only credential it carries is your short-lived sign-in token.
  • It never decides who may read something. Your agent decides, each time, against a live permission. (Evidence is never authority: a record saying someone is a friend grants nothing by itself.)
