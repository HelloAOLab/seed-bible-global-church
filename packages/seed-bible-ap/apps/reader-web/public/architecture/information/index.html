Architecture / Information Architecture

Information Architecture

Every piece of data the reader shows or keeps, sorted by where it lives and who owns it. The meaning of every record is defined in the ontology →

Where the data lives

1. Home 2. Personal context 3. Bible corpora 4. Published context 5. Publishing agent On the device only

One rule ties them together. Your own data is written only by your own agent, in your Home and Library. The reader and its workspace keep nothing of yours that counts; Bible text is never copied into your records (a mark points at a passage); and the serving plane is only an index, which can be rebuilt from the public shelves at any time.

1 Home — who you are

Your Home at www.faithnet.me holds your identity and your relationships. It is the same for every app you use, not only this reader. Apps may read some of it with your permission, but may never write it: when you change your name in the reader, the change is proposed to your Home, and the Home's own window saves it.

Data What it holds How the reader uses it
Profile First and last name, email, phone, location, organization, photo (your vault's impact-profile record) Reads your name and photo and shows them as you everywhere; proposes changes, which the Home saves
Name and identity Your <you>.me name on faithchain, your agent's account, the key that opens your vault The name every record is "held by"; the address a friend link carries
Contacts and friends People you added; a friend is someone you both added (role friend); contact notices Today's friends list, Friends pane, friend requests (person.contact.list, person.friend.list)
Invitations Friend invitations sent by email from the Home's own mail; whether people with your email may find you Add a friend by email; the welcome page an invitation opens
Presence Which friends are here now and where (one presence object per person) The green dot and "reading John 3 · BSB" in Today
Attestations Statements you affirmed once at your Home: the WEA Statement of Faith (the Home holds the exact text and its hash) and the Lausanne Covenant (read from the Lausanne Movement's signed release on the publishing agent). The record says "this person affirmed this exact text", with the time and the consent Not read by the reader yet. Any relying app can ask to see that you affirmed a statement without the Home re-sending the text
Affiliations and roles Churches and organizations you belong to, and the roles you hold in them "Publish as" a church; which church themes you may manage (person.affiliations.list, person.roles.list)
Connected assistants The permissions you gave apps and agents: the reader's "ask as me" wire, grants, consents The wire is what lets the workspace ask your agent to write your Library. You can revoke it here
Security Sign-in credentials and channels (passkeys, Google, email) Never seen by the reader; sign-in happens in the Home's window

2 Workspace — personal context

Everything you make while reading. It is kept in your Library (the file shelf at your Home), under the seed-bible/ folder, private unless you publish it. The reader writes it through the workspace, which asks your agent; it shows on the device first and syncs when you are signed in and online.

Marks (kept in "buckets" that merge without losing anything)

Data Type What it holds Kept as
Highlights seed:Highlight A colour on a verse or range, in a named translation One bucket per chapter
Notes seed:Annotation Rich text on a passage; tags, order, replies to another note One bucket per chapter
Bookmarks seed:Bookmark A named, coloured, movable place ("My bookmark") One bucket
Saves seed:Save, seed:SaveCategory A saved chapter or verse range, in a folder One bucket
Reading history seed:ReadingEvent A chapter you kept open about eight seconds; one per chapter per day One bucket per chapter
Plan progress seed:PlanProgress, seed:SessionProgress Where you are in each reading plan: cadence, time zone, completed readings One bucket

3 Scripture agent — the Bible corpora

The one source of Bible text and Bible data: scripture-resolver.svc at scripture.faithnet.io. Every edition is a signed corpus: each verse's wording has a fingerprint, the fingerprints roll up to one root per edition, and the agent signs an attestation of that root. The reader checks each chapter against it on your device. The reader only reads here; it never writes.

4 Workspace — published context (serving plane)

Anything you or a church chooses to share. Publishing does two things: your agent copies the item to your public shelf (seed-bible/public/<kind>/ in your Library, as JSON-LD plus a readable Markdown page) and signs a release of it. The record stays yours. The serving plane (seed-bible.svc) then finds it and indexes it so others can discover it.

What can be published

Kind Type Who publishes it Where others see it
Published notes seed:Annotation as an offering A reader Discover for the chapter; friends' Today feed; a share link
Playlists seed:Playlist A reader or church Discover; Library; /shared/… page ("Save to my Library and play")
Reading plans seed:ReadingPlan A reader or church Library; shared page ("Save to my Library and start")
Church themes seed:Customization A church, as its own agent Shared page ("Use this theme"); a customization link
Offerings seed:Offering (video, article, episode, study, image) A church or ministry Discover, for the passage it is about
Profile and picture seed:Profile, seed:GalleryImage A reader Friends see your name and picture

What the serving plane keeps

Data What it is Can it be lost?
Seed registry An index of every verified public release, by the passage it is about. Rebuilt by crawling the public shelves every 15 minutes No harm: rebuilt from the shelves
Public file copies Fast copies of published pictures (R2 seed-bible-files) No harm: refilled from the Libraries
Link previews Title and image for links in playlists and notes A cache
Map tiles Background map for the Scripture map A cache
Study companion and transcription AI answers about a passage; transcripts of recordings (Workers AI) Not stored as your record

Live reading sessions are a separate, short-lived room on the workspace: who is in it, the page, the chat. Nothing in the room is your record; only the host's session summary is kept, in their own Library. The town's public graph sees only the Markdown pages, never the structured records.

5 Publishing agent — complete works

Source Publishing at publishing.faithnet.io is a public archive of complete works: books, Bible translations, teaching series, articles and public-domain works. Each is published by its steward under their own Home identity, as a signed manifest that names every part by its fingerprint. So a quote can be checked against the work without trusting the gateway. Works can be free, or paid by credits or subscription (demo credits; no money moves).

Data What it holds Who uses it
Works and releases The signed manifest, the release, and the index of parts with their fingerprints Anyone; the Home and the workspace read them over A2A
Parts and blocks Each part's blocks with proofs that they belong to the release Reading a work; checking a quote
Readings by verse Every block, across works split by verse, that witnesses a verse. Today this is the Dead Sea Scroll lines: Hebrew, an English first draft, status, licence and a citation (verse, work, block, fingerprint) The reader's Content for a verse and Discover → Scrolls, read by verse and never copied
Quote checks Whether a quote is really the author's words, by fingerprint and proof Any app citing a work
Statements of faith For example the Lausanne Covenant, published by the Lausanne Movement Your Home, which reads the signed release before you affirm it (see Attestations above)

On the device only (for now)

Some things are preferences of one device, not records. They stay in the browser and do not follow you to another device yet. The ontology already has types for the first two (seed:ReaderSettings, seed:ReadingPosition), so they can move into the Library later.

  • Settings: theme, text size, fonts, line height, which elements show, interface language, toolbar layout.
  • Where you are: the open chapter, tabs and pane layout. (Recent reading across devices comes from your reading history, which is in your Library.)
  • Playlist play history: how far you got in each playlist.
  • Offline Bibles: downloaded translations and chapters you have opened.
  • The outbox: marks waiting to reach your Library.
